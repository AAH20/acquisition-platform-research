## Executive Summary

This research covers two distinct entities operating under similar names:

1. **Acquisition.com** (acquisition.com) — A private investment vehicle / family office founded by Alex & Leila Hormozi in 2021, based in Las Vegas, Nevada. Invests personal wealth in founder-led service-based businesses.

2. **Acquire.com** (acquire.com, formerly MicroAcquire) — A startup acquisition marketplace founded by Andrew Gazdecki in 2020, based in La Jolla, California. A Delaware corporation that operates a curated online marketplace for buying and selling online businesses.

Both entities operate in the M&A ecosystem but serve fundamentally different functions: Acquisition.com is an investment firm, while Acquire.com is a marketplace platform.

## 3. Licensing

### 3.1 M&A Broker Exemption (SEC)

The SEC provides an exemption from broker-dealer registration under Section 15(b) of the Exchange Act for M&A Brokers who facilitate:
- Mergers, acquisitions, business sales, and business combinations
- Between sellers and buyers of privately-held companies
- Without regard to the size of the privately-held company

**Conditions for M&A Broker Exemption:**
- The M&A Broker (and officers/directors/employees) must not be barred from association with a broker-dealer by the Commission, any state, or any self-regulatory organization
- Must not be suspended from association with a broker-dealer
- Permits advertising companies for sale with description, general location, and price range
- Permits participation in M&A Transactions with buyers formed without M&A Broker assistance

### 3.2 Family Office Exclusion (Investment Advisers Act of 1940)

Acquisition.com (Hormozi entity) benefits from the family office exclusion:
- Family offices are excluded from the definition of "investment adviser" under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940
- This avoids registration requirements and regulatory oversight that apply to firms managing other people's money
- Applies because the firm invests the Hormozis' personal wealth rather than pooling capital from outside investors

### 3.3 State-Level Considerations

- Acquisition.com: Nevada LLC (Nevada offers favorable LLC laws)
- Acquire.com: Delaware corporation (Delaware offers well-established corporate law)
- Both subject to federal securities laws regardless of state of incorporation

## 4. Securities Regulations

### 4.1 Applicable Federal Securities Laws

| Law | Relevance |
|-----|-----------|
| Securities Act of 1933 | Requires investors receive financial information concerning securities offered for public sale; prohibits deceit, misrepresentations, and fraud |
| Securities Exchange Act of 1934 | Regulates secondary trading; Williams Act amendments require mandatory disclosure of cash tender offers |
| Investment Advisers Act of 1940 | Regulates investment advisers; family office exclusion applies to Acquisition.com |
| Investment Company Act of 1940 | Regulates mutual funds and other investment companies |
| Trust Indenture Act of 1939 | Covers debt securities (bonds, debentures, notes) offered for public sale |

### 4.2 SEC Financial Disclosure Requirements for Acquisitions

**Regulation S-X Requirements:**
- **Rule 3-05:** Requires separate audited annual and unaudited interim pre-acquisition financial statements of acquired businesses (if significant)
- **Rule 3-14:** Requires financial statements for acquired real estate operations
- **Article 11:** Requires unaudited pro forma financial information showing how acquisition might have affected registrant's financial statements
- **Rule 1-02(w):** Defines "significant subsidiary" using investment, asset, and income tests (20% threshold for acquisitions)

**Amendments (Release No. 33-10786):**
- Financial statements of acquired business cover no more than two most recent fiscal years
- No longer require separate acquired business financial statements once included in registrant's audited financials for nine months or complete fiscal year
- Permit abbreviated financial statements for certain net asset acquisitions
- Permit use of IFRS as issued by IASB in certain circumstances

### 4.3 SBA 7(a) Loan Program Regulations

For acquisitions financed through SBA 7(a):
- Maximum loan size: $5M
- Down payment: 10% minimum (seller often holds standby note for additional 5%)
- Term: Up to 10 years (business acquisition without real estate); up to 25 years (with real estate)
- Rate: Prime + 2.25% to 2.75%
- Guarantee: SBA guarantees up to 75% of loan amount
- Buyer must operate business full-time post-close (passive investors do not qualify)
- Closing timelines: 60–120 days from LOI (vs. 30–60 for all-cash deals)

## 5. Dispute Resolution

### 5.1 Mandatory Binding Arbitration

Both Acquisition.com and Acquire.com Terms of Service mandate:

- **Arbitration Provider:** Judicial Arbitration and Mediation Services (JAMS)
- **Negotiation Period:** 15 days from notice of dispute before arbitration can be initiated
- **Binding Nature:** Final and binding settlement through arbitration
- **Individual Basis:** All claims must be arbitrated or litigated on an individual basis; no class or collective actions
- **Jury Trial Waiver:** Parties waive trial by jury of any dispute
- **Class Action Waiver:** Users cannot lead, join, or serve as representative of a class or group

### 5.2 Jurisdiction and Venue

- Legal actions must be brought in state or federal court located in Las Vegas (for Acquisition.com)
- Choice of law provisions favor the entity's home jurisdiction
- Arbitration awards can be enforced in court

### 5.3 Mass Arbitration Provisions

- If mass arbitration is triggered, JAMS shall manage the process
- Users acknowledge that mass arbitration might delay resolution of their dispute

### 5.4 Platform-Specific Dispute Context

**Common Disputes on Acquire.com:**
- LOI non-performance (buyers walking away after LOI submission)
- NDA harvesting by non-serious buyers
- Seller financial misrepresentation
- Post-close transition disputes
- Platform dependency transfer failures

**Mitigation Mechanisms:**
- On-platform document signing (reduces signing errors)
- Escrow protection through Escrow.com
- Funds verification for buyers
- Automated NDA enforcement
- Platform flagging of unusual legal document terms

## 7. Legal Bottlenecks

### 7.1 Structural Bottlenecks

| Bottleneck | Description | Impact |
|------------|-------------|--------|
| LOI Non-Binding Nature | Letters of Intent are mostly non-binding; buyers can walk away at any point | Creates uncertainty for sellers; wastes time and resources |
| Seller Verification Gap | Platform verifies buyer funds but does not audit seller financials as rigorously | Risk of misrepresented business metrics; buyer diligence burden |
| Generic APA Templates | Standard templates may not suit complex transactions | Reps & warranties, indemnification caps, escrow holdbacks, working capital adjustments, earnout language need customization |
| Customer Concentration | Many micro SaaS deals have top customer at 30–60% of revenue | Deal risk; financing challenges |
| Key Person Risk | Founder may be sole person with engineering knowledge | Post-close operational risk |
| Platform Dependency Transfer | SaaS built on Stripe, Shopify, Google Workspace, personal AWS | Transfer failures can unwind deals at last minute |
| Post-Close Transition | 30–90 day standard transitions; some longer | Inadequate transitions lead to business deterioration |

### 7.2 Process Bottlenecks

- **NDA Harvesting:** Buyers sign NDAs on multiple listings without intent to acquire, researching market instead
- **Lowball Offers:** Initial offers 30–50% below asking price common; lack of competing bids means no upward pressure
- **Slow Timelines:** 3–6 months from listing to close common; some deals never close
- **Off-Platform Signing Requests:** Buyers asking to go off-platform to sign legal docs is a red flag
- **SBA Financing Delays:** 60–120 day closing timelines with SBA vs. 30–60 for all-cash

### 7.3 Regulatory Bottlenecks

- **M&A Broker Exemption Limitations:** Conditions on size, manner of participation, and advertising must be carefully navigated
- **Family Office Exclusion Constraints:** Must maintain exclusive investment of family wealth; outside capital triggers registration
- **Securities Law Compliance:** Even private company transfers may implicate securities laws if structured as securities offerings
- **State Blue Sky Laws:** State-level securities regulations may apply regardless of federal exemptions

## 8. NP-Hard Problems in Acquisition Platform Operations

### 8.1 Deal Matching Optimization

The problem of optimally matching buyers to sellers in an acquisition marketplace is computationally complex:
- Multi-dimensional matching (industry, size, price range, geography, strategic fit)
- Incomplete information (buyers and sellers have private valuations)
- Dynamic marketplace (listings and buyers change over time)
- Constraint satisfaction (financing requirements, timing, deal structure preferences)

**Complexity Class:** NP-Hard (reducible to weighted bipartite matching with constraints)

### 8.2 Due Diligence Verification

Verifying seller claims across multiple dimensions:
- Financial statement accuracy (revenue, expenses, EBITDA)
- Customer concentration analysis
- Technology stack verification
- Legal compliance (contracts, IP, litigation)
- Market position assessment

**Complexity Class:** NP-Hard (multi-criteria decision analysis with incomplete information)

### 8.3 Valuation Determination

Determining fair market value for private businesses:
- Multiple valuation methodologies (DCF, comparable transactions, asset-based)
- Adjustments for market conditions, growth rates, risk factors
- Synergy valuation for strategic buyers
- Earnout and contingent consideration structuring

**Complexity Class:** NP-Hard (combinatorial optimization with uncertain inputs)

### 8.4 Risk Assessment

Comprehensive risk evaluation:
- Customer concentration risk (Pareto analysis)
- Key person dependency risk
- Platform/technology transfer risk
- Regulatory compliance risk
- Market/competitive risk

**Complexity Class:** NP-Hard (multi-objective optimization under uncertainty)
