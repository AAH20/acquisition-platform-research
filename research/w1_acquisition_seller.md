## 1. Onboarding Flow

Acquisition.com's seller onboarding is a guided, multi-step process designed to vet and prepare sellers before their listing goes live.

### Step-by-Step Flow

| Step | Action | Details |
|------|--------|---------|
| 1 | **Sign up** | Navigate to acquire.com/sellers, click "Join Now" |
| 2 | **Email verification** | Standard email verification |
| 3 | **Select intent** | Click "I want to sell my startup" |
| 4 | **Guided listing begins** | Click "Awesome, let's go" to start the guided process |
| 5 | **Basics** | Select startup type, enter name, enter valid website |
| 6 | **Website validation** | Platform validates website + SSL certificate; failure requires retry or support contact |
| 7 | **Seller type** | Select "As an individual" or "As a company" |
| 8 | **Seller details** | Enter appropriate information for individual or company |
| 9 | **Metrics connection** | Connect customer metrics (Stripe, PayPal, Braintree, App Stores) via ChartMogul, or web traffic via Google Analytics; manual entry available but discouraged |
| 10 | **Pre-approval review** | Curation team reviews basic details + asking price to determine marketplace fit |
| 11 | **Listing goes live** | Pre-approved listings are published quickly |

### Key Onboarding Facts

- **Rejection rate:** ~55% of seller applications are rejected; ~45% are approved (per Acquire.com's own published data)
- **Review speed:** Days (not weeks) — significantly faster than competitors like Empire Flippers (3-4 weeks)
- **No hard minimum valuation** — but SaaS/tech-first businesses with demonstrable traction are strongly preferred
- **Pre-revenue startups** face stricter scrutiny; demand fluctuates
- **Financial data connection is optional** but highly recommended for buyer trust
- **No exclusivity contract** — sellers can list elsewhere simultaneously
- **Free to list** — no upfront cost (though a monthly listing fee was introduced in April 2024)

### Seller Eligibility Criteria

| Criterion | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Business type | SaaS, ecommerce, agencies, content sites, mobile apps, service businesses with digital component |
| Revenue | Preferred but not strictly required; $5K+ MRR ideal |
| Profitability | SDE/EBITDA profitable (nearly all closed deals are profitable businesses) |
| Track record | 12+ months preferred; shorter history considered for strong MRR |
| Typical deal size | $10K–$10M enterprise value; sweet spot $100K–$3M |
| Geography | 100+ countries served; US-centric |

## 2. Listing Process

The listing creation process is a 9-step guided workflow that produces a two-column listing: public (left) and private/NDA-gated (right).

### The 9-Step Listing Creation Process

| Step | Component | Details |
|------|-----------|---------|
| 1 | **Tell us about your startup** | Basic details + asking price → triggers pre-approval |
| 2 | **Complete seller profile** | Seller background and experience |
| 3 | **Verify identity** | ID verification for seller authenticity |
| 4 | **General startup info** | Headline, asking price reasoning |
| 5 | **Company overview** | Description, business model, tech stack, competitors, growth opportunity, key assets, keywords |
| 6 | **Acquisition details** | Reason for selling, financing details |
| 7 | **Connect metrics** | Financial metrics, customer metrics, web traffic (via integrations) |
| 8 | **P&L statement** | Upload profit & loss statement |
| 9 | **Supporting documents** | Additional files, media, data room content |

### Listing Structure

- **Left column (public):** Visible to all buyers — category, geography, business model, asking price band, high-level metrics (MRR/revenue range, profit margin, team size, age)
- **Right column (private):** NDA-gated — detailed metrics, actual business name/URL, customer count, churn, traffic sources, seller's growth narrative and risk assessment

### Listing Optimization Best Practices

| Practice | Impact |
|----------|--------|
| Lead with traction (TTM revenue, MRR, margin %, YoY growth) | Grabs buyer attention faster than vision statements |
| Describe product in one plain sentence | Clarity beats jargon |
| Show real metrics (revenue, profit, churn, customer count) | Transparency builds trust |
| Upload visual assets (slides, screenshots, Loom walkthrough) | Builds trust faster, speeds offers |
| Connect Stripe/analytics for verification | Revenue verified before buyer sees page |
| State 12+ months of consistent MRR | Consistency signals real business |
| Include cohort churn data | Each 1% churn reduction can increase valuation 15-25% |
| Proactively disclose weaknesses | Builds credibility vs. burying in due diligence |
| Set acquisition timeline with deadlines | Prevents deal stalling |

### What Kills a Listing

- Missing numbers (opacity kills trust)
- Overpriced with no justification
- No clear product-market fit
- Confusing buzzwords
- Vague, buzzwordy descriptions
- Rushed or incomplete listings

### Listing Refresh

- Available if fewer than 5 buyers reach out in first 14 days
- Bumps listing to top of search results
- Alerts new buyers via matching engine
- Tracks buyer feedback on asking price
- If multiple buyers say price is too high, suggests pricing adjustment
- 10%+ discount triggers email to all interested buyers

## 6. Bottlenecks

### Structural Bottlenecks

| Bottleneck | Impact | Severity |
|------------|--------|----------|
| **Passive marketplace model** | No urgency or bidding pressure; if listing doesn't stand out in first 1-2 weeks, engagement drops sharply | High |
| **Ghosting after LOI** | Most common complaint; LOI is non-binding; buyers walk during due diligence | High |
| **NDA harvesting** | Buyers sign NDAs on multiple listings with no intent to acquire — researching market, not buying | High |
| **Lowball first offers** | Many initial offers 30–50% below asking; no competing bids means no upward pressure | Medium |
| **Long deal timelines** | 3–6 months from listing to close is common; some never close | Medium |
| **Buyer subscription barrier** | $390–$780/year buyer subscription limits total buyer pool; best buyers at premium tiers | Medium |
| **Category mismatch** | Content sites, bots, extensions, non-tech businesses have no natural buyer constituency | High |
| **Pre-revenue filter** | Platform moved decisively toward revenue-generating businesses; pre-revenue MVPs unlikely to produce results | Medium |
| **Upmarket shift** | Platform moving toward larger deals; smaller deals underserved | Medium |
| **No crypto support** | Limits payment flexibility | Low |

### Process Bottlenecks

| Bottleneck | Details |
|------------|---------|
| **Due diligence complexity** | Most challenging stage; sellers often overwhelmed; requires organized data room |
| **Seller financing friction** | Only deal component that can't transact on Escrow.com; manual process |
| **Off-platform risk** | Closing off-platform to avoid closing fee is a common pitfall (and violation) |
| **Legal complexity** | Asset vs. stock acquisition, purchase price allocation, tax obligations — sellers need counsel |
| **Asset transfer coordination** | Must inventory all assets, describe destination, explain transfer process; transfer one at a time |
| **Keyman risk** | Businesses dependent on one individual are less valuable; mitigation takes 3–5 years |

### Seller-Reported Frustrations

> "I got 15 NDAs and zero serious offers." — High NDA volume with low LOI conversion

> "The buyer disappeared after the LOI." — Non-binding LOI creates false sense of progress

> "My listing has been up for four months with no movement." — Passive model means stale listings sit indefinitely

> "The success fee stung on a small deal." — 6–8% closing fee + monthly listing fee adds up

## 7. NP-Hard Problems

Several aspects of the Acquisition.com seller experience involve computationally hard optimization problems:

### 7.1 Valuation Pricing Optimization

**Problem:** Determine optimal asking price maximizing expected sale price × probability of sale.

**Why NP-Hard:** Multi-variable optimization across revenue, growth rate, margins, churn, customer concentration, market conditions, comparable deals, and buyer sentiment. The valuation tool uses proprietary data from thousands of exits, but the full problem involves:
- Non-linear relationships between metrics and multiples
- Market timing dynamics
- Buyer-specific utility functions
- Competitive listing landscape

**Current Approach:** Simplified valuation tool (few inputs → market-based range) + M&A advisor adjustment. Sellability Score (ML) in development to predict sale likelihood.

### 7.2 Buyer-Seller Matching

**Problem:** Match sellers with the optimal set of buyers to maximize competitive dynamics and close probability.

**Why NP-Hard:** Combinatorial matching problem with constraints:
- Buyer criteria (category, size, geography, budget)
- Seller requirements (price, deal structure, timeline)
- Compatibility scoring across multiple dimensions
- Optimal portfolio allocation for buyers (each buyer has limited capital)

**Current Approach:** Seller Invitations (notify sellers of matching buyers), Buyer Concierge, matching engine for listing refresh. No automated optimal matching.

### 7.3 Deal Structure Optimization

**Problem:** Determine optimal deal structure (cash vs. seller financing vs. earnout vs. holdback) maximizing seller risk-adjusted proceeds.

**Why NP-Hard:** Trade-off space includes:
- Tax implications (asset vs. stock, capital gains timing)
- Risk allocation (escrow holdbacks, earnouts)
- Buyer financing constraints
- Time value of money
- Probability-weighted outcomes

**Current Approach:** LOI/APA builders with templates; M&A advisor guidance; no automated optimization.

### 7.4 Timing Optimization

**Problem:** Determine optimal time to list (market conditions, business momentum, personal readiness) to maximize exit value.

**Why NP-Hard:** Requires predicting:
- Market multiple fluctuations
- Buyer pool depth at future time
- Business trajectory (growth vs. decay)
- Competitive landscape evolution
- Macroeconomic conditions

**Current Approach:** Educational guidance ("list while business has momentum"); Acquisition Multiples Report for market context; no predictive timing tool.

### 7.5 Portfolio Allocation (Buyer Side)

**Problem:** Buyers with fixed capital must select optimal portfolio of acquisitions.

**Why NP-Hard:** Knapsack-like problem with:
- Synergy effects between acquisitions
- Risk diversification constraints
- Operational capacity limits
- Return target requirements

**Current Approach:** Buyer self-service; no portfolio optimization tool.
