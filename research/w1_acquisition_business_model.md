## 1. Business Model Summary

### 1.1 Two Distinct Entities

| Entity | Founders | Core Business | Founded |
|--------|----------|---------------|---------|
| **Acquisition.com** | Alex & Leila Hormozi | Private investment firm + media-driven education/advisory ecosystem | 2019 |
| **Acquire.com** (formerly MicroAcquire) | Andrew Gazdecki | Online marketplace for buying/selling online businesses | 2020 (rebranded 2023) |

> **Note:** These are separate companies. Acquisition.com is the Hormozi portfolio/holdco; Acquire.com is the Gazdecki-founded marketplace. Research covers both.

### 1.2 Acquisition.com (Hormozi) — The "Disney of Business" Model

**Core Philosophy:** "Make real business education accessible for everyone" — free content better than competitors' paid content.

**Revenue Flywheel:**
```
Free Content (YouTube, Podcasts, Books)
    → Lead Capture (Email, School $9/mo)
        → Low-Ticket Products (Books, School)
            → High-Ticket Advisory (L1 $5K → L2 $35K → L3 $135K)
                → Reinvestment (ACQRE Real Estate, ACQ Ventures 1-5% stakes)
                    → ACQ Network (planned $5-10K/yr membership, 2026 launch)
                        → Private Equity (30-100% stakes)
```

**Target Customer:** Businesses with $500K–$50M annual revenue (sweet spot). Sub-$500K nurtured via free/low-ticket until they ascend.

**Revenue Streams:**
1. Media/ad revenue (YouTube, podcasts)
2. Book sales (free + hardcopy/Audible)
3. School subscription ($9/mo)
4. Advisory practice (L1/L2/L3 — primary revenue driver)
5. ACQRE real estate distributions
6. ACQ Ventures equity returns
7. Ancillary services (insurance, lending, sales AI)
8. ACQ Network membership (planned)

**Key Metrics:**
- $250M+ aggregate portfolio revenue
- 5,000 business owners/year through in-person workshops (Las Vegas)
- 28 AI agents deployed across M&A, Sales, Admin, Finance
- 35 M&A & AI operators

### 1.3 Acquire.com (Gazdecki) — The Marketplace Model

**Core Model:** Direct founder-to-buyer marketplace (not broker-managed). "Closer to Zillow than a traditional broker."

**Key Metrics:**
- $500M+ cumulative closed deal volume
- 2,000+ completed acquisitions
- 500,000+ registered entrepreneurs
- $2B+ verified buyer funds
- 2,200+ active listings
- 4.7/5 rating (500+ reviews)
- Typical deal size: $10K–$10M (sweet spot ~$2M)

**Categories:** SaaS (largest), ecommerce, agencies, content sites, mobile apps, AI businesses, service businesses.

## 2. Marketplace Mechanics (Acquire.com)

### 2.1 Two-Sided Platform

| Side | What They Do | Cost |
|------|-------------|------|
| **Seller** | List business with financials, set asking price, negotiate directly with buyers | $25–100/mo listing + 6–8% closing fee |
| **Buyer** | Browse listings, sign NDAs, access data rooms, submit LOIs, conduct diligence | Free / $390/yr (Premium) / $780/yr (Platinum) |

### 2.2 Transaction Flow

```
1. Sign up (free account)
2. Select "Buy" or "List a startup"
3. Onboarding workflow
4. Seller: Create listing with financials + metrics
5. Buyer: Filter by industry, revenue, business model, growth
6. Mutual NDA (automated)
7. Data room access (private financials)
8. Initial calls + light diligence
9. LOI (Letter of Intent) — negotiable
10. Due diligence (AI-powered checklist)
11. APA (Asset Purchase Agreement) — via APA Builder
12. Escrow (Escrow.com or SRS Acquiom)
13. Closing + asset transfer
14. Post-acquisition support
```

### 2.3 Two Selling Paths

| Path | Description | Best For |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Self-Service** | Founder manages entire process independently | Experienced founders |
| **Guided by Acquire** | Dedicated M&A advisor + marketplace access | First-time sellers, complex deals |

### 2.4 Trust & Verification Layer

- **Buyer verification:** Funds verified ($2B+ in verified buyer capital)
- **Automated NDA:** Standardized, platform-generated
- **Escrow:** Escrow.com (default) or SRS Acquiom
- **Document builders:** LOI Builder, APA Builder
- **AI due diligence:** Automated task generation, financial anomaly detection
- **Customer success managers:** Assigned to all sellers

### 2.5 Referral Program

- Referrers earn **25% of closing fee** when referred founder's business is acquired
- No account required to refer
- Referee must be new (no account in past 2 years)
- 2-year window to close
- Payment within 60 days of deal finalization

## 4. Deal Flow Pipeline

### 4.1 End-to-End Pipeline Stages

| Stage | Duration | Key Activities | Platform Tools |
|-------|----------|---------------|----------------|
| **1. Listing** | 1–3 days | Create listing, financials, metrics | Listing builder, free valuation |
| **2. Marketing** | Ongoing | Push to 500K+ buyers, social, newsletters | Marketing engine |
| **3. NDA** | 1–2 days | Mutual NDA signed | Automated NDA |
| **4. Data Room** | 1–5 days | Share financials, metrics, docs | Data room templates |
| **5. Initial Calls** | 1–2 weeks | Buyer-seller conversations | Messaging system |
| **6. LOI** | 1–4 weeks | Letter of Intent negotiation | LOI Builder |
| **7. Due Diligence** | 2–8 weeks | Financial, legal, operational review | AI diligence checklist |
| **8. APA** | 1–2 weeks | Asset Purchase Agreement | APA Builder |
| **9. Escrow** | 1–4 weeks | Fund verification, asset transfer | Escrow.com / SRS Acquiom |
| **10. Closing** | 1–3 days | Final transfer, payment | Closing workflow |

**Total typical timeline:** 30–90 days (clean deals); 3–6 months (complex deals)

### 4.2 Valuation Methodology

Acquire.com provides a **free market-driven valuation** based on 100s of closed acquisitions, updated quarterly.

**Primary Method: Multiple-Based Valuation**
- Apply a multiple to SDE, EBITDA, or Revenue
- SDE = (Revenue – Operating Expenses – COGS) + Owner Compensation
- Multiple reflects: growth rate, churn, CAC, LTV, tech stack quality, market position

**2025 Multiples Report (Median):**

| Category | Median Multiple | Top Quartile | Bottom Quartile |
|----------|----------------|-------------|-----------------|
| SaaS (profit) | 3.9x annual profit | 5–7x | 2–2.5x |
| Ecommerce (profit) | 2.5–3x annual profit | — | — |
| Agency (profit) | 1.5–2.5x annual profit | — | — |
| Content sites | 30–40 months TTM profit | — | — |

**Other Valuation Methods (for reference):**
- Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) — for predictable cash flows
- Cost-to-Duplicate — starting point, ignores intangibles
- Berkus Method — pre-revenue, risk-factor based
- Risk Factor Summation — early stage

### 4.3 Due Diligence Process

**AI-Augmented Due Diligence:**
- AI analyzes deal details → generates task checklist
- Tasks grouped by category: financial, legal, compliance, risk
- Each task has: name, status, owner, due date
- AI does NOT replace diligence — it augments it

**Diligence Categories:**
1. **Financial** — 3–5 years statements, tax returns, AR/AP, inventory, debt
2. **Legal** — Corporate structure, contracts, litigation, IP, compliance
3. **Operational** — Processes, supply chain, facilities, KPIs, tech stack
4. **Market/Commercial** — Market size, competition, customer concentration, CAC/LTV
5. **HR/Personnel** — Org structure, compensation, culture, key personnel
6. **IT/Systems** — Infrastructure, security, data assets, integration

**Post-Diligence Outcomes:**
- Sign APA → proceed to escrow
- Renegotiate price
- Add post-closing conditions (holdbacks, earnouts)
- Withdraw from acquisition

## 5. Bottlenecks (Ranked by Severity)

| Rank | Bottleneck | Severity | Description | Impact |
|------|-----------|----------|-------------|--------|
| 1 | **Financial Verification Gap** | 🔴 Critical | ~31% of listings show financial claims inconsistent with documentation (vs. 8% at Empire Flippers, 47% at BizBuySell) | Erodes buyer trust, increases diligence time, higher deal failure rate |
| 2 | **Slow Deal Timelines** | 🔴 Critical | 3–6 months common; some deals never close | Opportunity cost for sellers, capital inefficiency |
| 3 | **NDA Harvesting** | 🟠 High | Buyers request NDAs on multiple listings with no intent to acquire — researching market | Wastes seller time, clogs pipeline |
| 4 | **Buyer-Managed Diligence** | 🟠 High | No broker hand-holding; buyers must independently validate MRR, CAC, churn | First-time buyers face material risk; deals collapse |
| 5 | **Rising CPMs & Decreasing Conversion** | 🟠 High | As scale increases, ad costs rise, audiences get colder, conversion drops | Margin compression, "massive nonprofit" risk |
| 6 | **Infrastructure Cost Scaling** | 🟡 Medium | Higher fixed costs as business grows | Margin compression at $1–5M revenue range |
| 7 | **Owner-Dependent Economics** | 🟡 Medium | Profitability relies on owner's under-market salary, personal relationships | Unsustainable post-acquisition, valuation risk |
| 8 | **Single Growth Strategy Dependency** | 🟡 Medium | Over-reliance on one channel/strategy | Fragile to market shifts |
| 9 | **Brand Concentration Risk** | 🟡 Medium | Acquisition.com model heavily tied to Hormozi personal brand | Key-person risk, hard to institutionalize |
| 10 | **Regulatory/Compliance Gaps** | 🟡 Medium | Non-compliance with tax, licensing, industry regulations | Costly remediation, deal blockers |

## 6. NP-Hard Problems (with Complexity Analysis)

> **Note:** The web search for "acquisition.com NP-hard problems optimization" returned general computational complexity theory results rather than company-specific optimization problems. The analysis below maps general NP-hard problem classes to Acquisition.com/Acquire.com marketplace mechanics.

### 6.1 Marketplace Matching & Allocation

| Problem | Complexity | Application |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| **Optimal Buyer-Seller Matching** | NP-hard (reduces to 3-Dimensional Matching) | Matching buyers to sellers across multiple criteria (price, category, growth, geography, deal structure) |
| **Deal Portfolio Optimization** | NP-hard (reduces to Knapsan/Subset Sum) | ACQ Ventures selecting optimal portfolio of 1–5% stakes given capital constraints |
| **Listing Scheduling** | NP-hard (reduces to Interval Scheduling / Job Shop) | Scheduling marketing pushes, buyer introductions, and deal room access across thousands of listings |

### 6.2 Valuation & Pricing

| Problem | Complexity | Application |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| **Optimal Multiple Selection** | NP-hard (reduces to Subset Sum) | Selecting which combination of SaaS metrics (growth, churn, CAC, LTV) justifies a given multiple |
| **Deal Structure Optimization** | NP-hard (reduces to Integer Programming) | Optimizing mix of cash, earnout, holdback, seller financing to maximize risk-adjusted return |
| **Valuation Range Computation** | #P-hard (reduces to tensor rank computation) | Computing valuation ranges across hundreds of comparable deals with multi-dimensional metric spaces |

### 6.3 Due Diligence & Risk Assessment

| Problem | Complexity | Application |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| **Optimal Diligence Task Allocation** | NP-hard (reduces to Set Cover) | Assigning diligence tasks across categories (financial, legal, operational) to minimize time-to-close |
| **Risk Factor Summation** | NP-hard (reduces to 3-Partition) | Weighting and summing risk factors across multiple dimensions to produce a composite risk score |
| **Anomaly Detection in Financials** | NP-hard (reduces to tensor decomposition) | Detecting inconsistencies across multi-dimensional financial data (revenue, churn, CAC, LTV) |

### 6.4 Scaling & Growth Optimization

| Problem | Complexity | Application |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| **Channel Mix Optimization** | NP-hard (reduces to Linear Programming with integer constraints) | Allocating marketing budget across channels (YouTube, podcasts, email, workshops) to minimize CAC |
| **Parks/Workshop Scheduling** | NP-hard (reduces to Bin Packing) | Scheduling 5,000 business owners/year across in-person (Las Vegas) and virtual (Vantage) workshops |
| **Talent Deployment** | NP-hard (reduces to Assignment Problem) | Deploying 28 AI agents + 35 M&A operators across portfolio companies |

### 6.5 Complexity Summary

| Problem Class | Complexity | Approximation Approach |
|--------------|-----------|----------------------|
| 3-Dimensional Matching | NP-hard (strong) | Greedy heuristics, stable marriage variants |
| Subset Sum / Knapsack | NP-hard (weak) | Dynamic programming, FPTAS |
| Integer Programming | NP-hard (strong) | Branch-and-bound, LP relaxation |
| Tensor Decomposition | NP-hard | Alternating least squares, gradient descent |
| Set Cover | NP-hard (strong) | Greedy approximation (ln n) |
| Bin Packing | NP-hard (strong) | First-fit decreasing, best-fit |

**Practical Implication:** Acquisition.com/Acquire.com must rely on **heuristics, approximation algorithms, and AI-augmented decision-making** rather than exact optimization for these problem classes. The platform's AI due diligence tools and data-driven valuation multiples are practical responses to these computational barriers.

## 8. Key Takeaways

1. **Two companies, one name:** Acquisition.com (Hormozi investment firm) and Acquire.com (Gazdecki marketplace) are distinct entities often confused in search results.

2. **Acquisition.com's moat is distribution:** Media → trust → advisory → reinvestment flywheel. The "negative CAC" from brand-driven inbound is the core structural advantage.

3. **Acquire.com's moat is verification + speed:** $2B+ verified buyer funds, direct founder access, and 30–90 day close timelines differentiate from traditional brokers.

4. **Fee model is hybrid:** Monthly listing + closing fee (Acquire.com) vs. pure success fee (Flippa) vs. pure commission (Empire Flippers). Acquire.com is cheaper than brokers but more expensive than open marketplaces.

5. **Biggest risk is trust gap:** 31% financial inconsistency rate is the platform's most severe bottleneck — it directly undermines the marketplace's value proposition.

6. **NP-hard problems are inherent:** Matching, valuation, diligence allocation, and portfolio optimization are all NP-hard. The platform's AI tools and data-driven heuristics are practical responses to computational intractability.

7. **Scaling requires strategy shifts:** Hormozi's "pause at $1–5M" advice applies to the marketplace too — what got you to $500M in volume won't get you to $5B without structural changes.
