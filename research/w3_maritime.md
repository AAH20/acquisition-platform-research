# Wave 3 Research: Dual-Use Maritime & Naval Technology Acquisitions

**Date:** 2026-10-05  
**Scope:** Dual-use maritime and naval technology M&A, valuation, bottlenecks, NP-hard problems, and technology transfer

---

## Market Overview

The global maritime and naval M&A landscape is experiencing a structural upcycle driven by geopolitical tensions, European rearmament, and the rapid maturation of autonomous maritime systems. Key market dynamics include:

- **A&D M&A Surge:** Worldwide aerospace & defense M&A deal announcements increased 41% in 2025 to 532 transactions, with aggregate deal value surging 60% to $42.7 billion. Q1 2026 continued momentum with volume up 37% and value up 166% YoY. Europe is emerging as the engine of growth, outpacing North America as investors anticipate sustained rearmament spending.
- **Naval M&A Heating Up:** Defense dealmaking is moving toward maritime layers that make fleets harder to blind, harder to surprise, and faster to regenerate. The hottest targets are not platform builders but companies owning bottlenecks: detection, navigation, autonomy, mission software, communications, repair, propulsion, cyber, and sustainment.
- **Dual-Use Procurement Innovation:** The U.S. Navy's MUSV Marketplace and the merged Modular Attack Surface Craft (MASC) program represent a paradigm shift—leveraging Other Transaction Authority (OTA) agreements to rapidly procure commercial-ready autonomous vessels. Saronic, HII, and Galliano Marine Services each received contracts for 10 MUSVs at ~$40M per vessel, with deliveries by end of FY2027.
- **EU Maritime Strategy:** The EU Industrial Maritime Strategy explicitly identifies dual-use vessels, platforms, and technologies as growth vectors, proposing a Dual-Use Ferry Construction Support Mechanism and emphasizing military mobility, Arctic security, and critical infrastructure protection.
- **Supply Chain Dominance:** Supply chain was the single most active A&D M&A theme in 2025, totaling ~$14.6 billion in transactions. Buyers are acquiring capacity to protect production schedules and secure scarce inputs.

## Key Technologies

### 1. Undersea Warfare Systems
- Sonar, sonobuoys, torpedo defense, UUVs, and underwater sensing are moving from niche naval assets to top acquisition priorities.
- Lockheed Martin's deeper push into undersea warfare via Ultra Maritime; Thales moving toward Exail's maritime robotics and inertial navigation stack; Fincantieri building an underwater portfolio across drones, subsea communications, and survey.
- Full chain attractiveness: acoustic sensors, sonobuoy production, signal processing, torpedo countermeasures, autonomous sensing platforms, and data links into combat systems.

### 2. Maritime Robotics (USV/UUV)
- Mine countermeasures, seabed mapping, surveillance, harbor defense, decoy operations, logistics, and distributed sensing are pulling unmanned vessels into mainstream naval procurement.
- Strongest targets: companies with vehicles, payload integration, launch/recovery knowledge, autonomy software, support models, and naval testing evidence.
- U.S. Navy's Overlord program and MASC program are accelerating the transition to a hybrid manned-unmanned fleet.

### 3. Autonomy & Mission Control
- Defense primes want software and navigation layers enabling unmanned surface, undersea, and aerial systems to operate as part of a fleet.
- Mission planning, multi-drone control, sensor fusion, AI decision support, cyber resilience, and human-machine teaming.
- Higher margins than hardware-heavy assets; harder diligence because demo performance may not equal fleet adoption.

### 4. Seabed Awareness & Critical Infrastructure Protection
- Subsea cables, pipelines, and offshore energy assets are driving government demand for persistent underwater monitoring and data fusion.
- Seabed mapping around undersea cables, pipelines, and other critical infrastructure for maintenance and security.

### 5. Resilient Navigation & Cyber/Electronic Warfare
- Technologies enabling operations without GPS, through jamming, and in contested electromagnetic environments.
- NATO DIANA highlights resilient and adaptable USVs/UUVs manufactured using rapid, cheap mass production techniques.

### 6. Repair & Sustainment Technology
- Dry dock access, specialty trades, propulsion support, additive manufacturing, repair analytics, and parts forecasting.
- Readiness depends on ships returning to service quickly; repair capacity is becoming a strategic asset.

### 7. Energy Solutions for Maritime Environments
- Energy solutions ruggedized against biological fouling and saltwater ingress.
- Technologies exploiting local power sources such as wave and tidal power.

## Valuation

### A&D Sector Valuation Trends
- Global A&D deal value rose from ~$27B (2024) to >$30B (2025), with H1 2026 tracking toward a similar full-year pace.
- Capital is concentrating around defense technology, space, autonomy, and production-constrained assets; legacy complexity faces growing valuation pressure.
- Carve-outs, vertical integration, and next-gen defense technology are driving premium valuations.
- The top five A&D primes ended FY2025 with $1.36 trillion in combined backlog, up 23.7% YoY.
- Dual-track processes are accelerating as strong investor demand drives higher multiples across strategic, PE, VC, and public markets.

### Maritime-Specific Valuation
- **Venture Capital:** VC investment in maritime technology grew from near-zero to ~$300M in 2018, with the first maritime unicorn (Flexport) emerging in 2019. Investors remain significantly underweight the sector relative to its share of global GDP—parity would require 3x the 2018 investment level annually.
- **Asset Valuation Platforms:** Maritime Strategies International launched MSI HORIZON, a digital platform providing proprietary macro/shipping market models and asset valuations. The Forward Marine eValuator (FMV) and Alternative Marine eValuator (AMV) enable stress-testing fleet valuations against scenarios (e.g., Suez closure, Chinese recession) up to 2035.
- **Scarcity Premium:** In European maritime M&A, scarcity increasingly determines strategic value. Companies with protected cargo access, scarce repair capacity, or difficult-to-replace engineering expertise command premiums over larger fleets exposed to the freight cycle.
- **USV Unit Economics:** MUSV procurement at ~$40M per vessel represents a new benchmark for medium autonomous platforms, with production OT agreements enabling faster scaling than traditional program-of-record approaches.

## Bottlenecks

### Administrative & Regulatory Bottlenecks
- **EU Shipyard Expansion:** Administrative permissions limit shipyards' ability to expand or modernize facilities or install decarbonization technologies (clean fuel bunkering infrastructure, dockside electrification). Proposed Regulation on speeding up environmental assessments aims to address this.
- **Export Controls & National Security Reviews:** ITAR, FOCI, and national security reviews make diligence and integration planning central to value creation in cross-border maritime deals.
- **Environmental Assessment Delays:** Lengthy environmental assessment processes constrain maritime infrastructure development.

### Industrial & Capacity Bottlenecks
- **Shipyard Capacity:** Limited dry dock access, specialty trades, and repair technology are becoming strategic bottlenecks. Readiness depends on ships returning to service quickly.
- **Supply Chain Fragility:** Supply chain was the dominant A&D M&A theme in 2025 (~$14.6B), reflecting acute scarcity of critical inputs and capacity.
- **Speed-Requirements Tension:** Increasing requirements for capabilities like speed create production challenges for industry and affect vessel designs. The Navy is seeking "the sweet spot" where healthy competition and dual-use potential intersect.

### Technology Adoption Bottlenecks
- **Push Barriers:** Low visibility of maritime sector among VCs/tech entrepreneurs; poor financial performance of shipowners; fragmentation of shipping segments.
- **Pull Barriers:** Poor internet connectivity of vessels; focus on asset trading returns rather than operational improvement.
- **Demo-to-Fleet Gap:** Autonomy software demo performance may not equal fleet adoption; harder diligence required for software-centric targets.

### Talent & Knowledge Bottlenecks
- Specialty trades, naval testing evidence, and production readiness are scarce and difficult to replicate.
- Family-controlled maritime businesses face growing cost of renewal, emissions compliance, cybersecurity, and succession challenges.

## NP-Hard Problems in Maritime

Maritime logistics and operations involve several computationally intractable problems that create both challenges and opportunities for technology acquisition targets.

### 1. Container Vessel Stowage Planning
- **Master Stowage Plan (MSP):** Minimizing lid overstows in master stowage plans for container vessels is **NP-complete** (proven by reduction from Minimum Set Cover). This is central to any successful approach to container vessel stowage.
- **Paired Block Stowage:** Searching in valid paired block stowage patterns is **NP-hard** (proven by reduction from Set Partitioning). Template planning formulations outperform traditional allocation models by 2-4.5x on larger instances.
- **RoRo Stowage Planning:** The slot-based RoRo stowage planning problem is **NP-complete** (proven by reduction from Stacking-No-Shift). The unrestricted version is NP-hard. Hard instances can be systematically generated for benchmarking.

### 2. Implications for Acquisition Targets
- Companies offering heuristic, approximation, or decomposition-based solutions to these NP-hard problems are valuable because exact optimization is intractable at scale.
- The computational complexity creates a moat: targets with proven, scalable stowage optimization algorithms have defensible technology positions.
- Hierarchical decomposition (master planning → slot planning) is the industry-standard approach, making companies with expertise in either layer attractive.

### 3. Other Hard Problems in Maritime Operations
- **Fleet Scheduling & Routing:** Multi-port, multi-vessel routing with time windows, capacity constraints, and uncertain demand is NP-hard.
- **Crane Scheduling:** Minimizing port stay through optimal crane assignment and scheduling is NP-hard.
- **Berth Allocation:** Allocating vessels to berths with spatial and temporal constraints is NP-hard.
