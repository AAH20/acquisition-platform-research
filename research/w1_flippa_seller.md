## 1. Onboarding Flow

Flippa's seller onboarding follows a structured multi-step journey:

| Step | Description | Key Details |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **1. Account Registration** | Create a free Flippa account | Quick sign-up; enables listing, tracking, buyer communication, and transaction completion |
| **2. ID Verification** | Mandatory identity verification | Completed via "My Profile" during listing creation or at sale completion; required for all sellers |
| **3. Business Valuation** | Free Intelligent Valuations Engine | ~5 minutes; uses 10+ years of transaction data; asks business type, URL, revenue, expenses, hours/week, traffic |
| **4. Listing Creation** | Step-by-step template | Guided form with 14+ data points including financials, analytics, description, images |
| **5. Financial Verification** | Connect data sources | Google Analytics, QuickBooks, Stripe, Shopify, Amazon, PayPal integrations; 12-month P&L required |
| **6. Go Live** | Listing published | Auction or fixed-price format; listing fee $10–$599 depending on asset type and package |
| **7. Post-Sale** | Escrow & asset transfer | Escrow.com integration; Post-Sales Completion Area for due diligence and transfer |

**Key Onboarding Insight:** The valuation step doubles as onboarding — sellers begin providing business data before they even decide to list, creating a natural funnel from "curious" to "committed."

## 2. Listing Process

### 2.1 Listing Types

| Type | Best For | Fee Structure |
|------|----------|---------------|
| **Auction** | Most assets; encourages competitive bidding | Listing fee + success fee (5–10%) |
| **Fixed Price** | Businesses valued >$100K | Listing fee + success fee |
| **Broker-Assisted** | 7-figure+ businesses | 15% success fee (shared with broker) |

1. Business nature and URL
2. Business type (eCommerce, SaaS, Services, Marketplace, Advertising)
3. Industry category (17 options)
4. Monetization methods (multiple allowed)
5. Google Analytics connection (2.8x more views, 2.4x more watchers)
6. 12-month revenue and expense history (month-by-month)
7. P&L statement (manual upload or QuickBooks connect)
8. Revenue evidence (screenshots, exports from Shopify/Amazon/PayPal)
9. Tagline (120 characters, keyword-optimized)
10. Detailed description (business model, operations, USP, transfer process)
11. Cover image (min 550×300px; recommended 1,340×580px)
12. Social media following, inventory, included assets
13. Auction vs. fixed-price selection with pricing
14. Post-sale support offering

## 6. Bottlenecks

### 6.1 Seller-Side Bottlenecks

| Bottleneck | Severity | Description |
|------------|----------|-------------|
| **Listing volume / discoverability** | Critical | Thousands of active listings; most expire without a single serious offer |
| **Unqualified buyer inquiries** | High | Significant time spent on tire-kickers, lowball offers, and dead-end conversations |
| **Verification gap** | High | Unverified listings treated with suspicion; two-tier trust dynamic |
| **Overpricing** | High | Sellers anchor to perceived value rather than market reality; overpriced listings ignored |
| **Weak listing quality** | Medium | Vague descriptions, missing financials, poor images reduce engagement |
| **Slow response time** | Medium | Deals lost to faster-responding sellers; 24–48h expected |
| **Transfer documentation gaps** | Medium | Unclear handover plans kill deals late in the process |
| **Niche asset invisibility** | Medium | Telegram bots, Chrome extensions, AI tools, newsletters get buried |
| **Fee stacking** | Medium | Listing fees + success fees + upgrade costs add up; no sale = sunk cost |
| **Support responsiveness** | Medium | BBB F rating; refund disputes; slow resolution |

### 6.2 Structural Bottlenecks

| Bottleneck | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| **Open marketplace model** | No inventory curation; quality varies wildly; sellers compete with unverified listings |
| **Self-reported data** | Many listings rely on unverified claims; buyers skeptical without third-party data |
| **Buyer quality variance** | Skews toward first-time acquirers; sophisticated buyers scarce for complex assets |
| **Search-based discovery** | Default is classified-ad model, not auction; less urgency/competition |
| **Geographic concentration** | Buyer pool concentrated in US/EU; cross-border deals face friction |

## 7. NP-Hard Problems

### 7.1 Fundamental Computational Challenges

| Problem | Complexity | Flippa's Approach |
|---------|------------|-------------------|
| **Optimal pricing** | NP-hard (market clearing with incomplete information) | Valuation engine with historical data; still requires seller judgment |
| **Buyer-seller matching** | NP-hard (stable marriage / assignment problem) | AI matching engine with 10M+ matches/month; heuristic-based |
| **Listing ranking** | NP-hard (multi-objective optimization) | Proprietary ranking algorithm weighing 10+ signals |
| **Fraud detection** | NP-hard (anomaly detection at scale) | Verification integrations, manual review, community reporting |
| **Valuation accuracy** | NP-hard (predicting future cash flows) | Data-driven multiples; 10+ years of transaction data |
| **Due diligence automation** | NP-hard (document understanding, risk assessment) | Data Room + manual review; AI-assisted CIM generation |

### 7.2 Why These Are Hard

1. **Information asymmetry:** Sellers know their business better than buyers; verifying claims is expensive
2. **Heterogeneous assets:** No two online businesses are identical; comparison is approximate
3. **Dynamic market:** Multiples shift with interest rates, platform changes, AI disruption
4. **Adverse selection:** Sellers with the most to hide are the least likely to verify
5. **Scalability vs. curation:** Open marketplace scales but sacrifices quality control

### 7.3 Implications for Acquisition Platform Design

- **Verification is the moat:** Platforms that solve verification at scale win trust and liquidity
- **AI matching > manual search:** Reduces discovery cost for both sides
- **Structured data > free text:** Standardized financials, SOPs, and metrics enable comparison
- **Escrow is table stakes:** Non-negotiable for cross-border digital asset transfer
- **Broker layer for high-value:** Complex deals need human judgment; self-serve works for lower tiers
