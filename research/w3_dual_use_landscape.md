## 4. Bottlenecks (Ranked by Severity)

### Severity 1 — CRITICAL: Valuation Gap & Affordability Crisis

- Median defense-tech pre-money valuation nearly doubled from $61M (2024) to $115M (2025).
- Anduril at $61B valuation (reportedly in talks at ~$100B) = ~45x revenue, while primes trade at low single-digit multiples.
- Castelion raised $1B+ at $13B valuation — too large for ordinary strategic tuck-in acquisitions.
- **Impact:** Reduces the number of corporations that can realistically acquire top defense-tech startups. Market may consolidate "from the middle" — smaller specialists get acquired while unicorns stay independent and become buyers themselves.

### Severity 2 — CRITICAL: Regulatory & Export Control Barriers (ITAR/EAR/FOCI)

- ITAR (22 C.F.R. Parts 120–130, DDTC) governs defense articles on USML.
- EAR (15 C.F.R. Parts 730–774, BIS) governs dual-use items on Commerce Control List.
- Foreign sovereign wealth fund LP bases create FOCI (Foreign Ownership, Control, or Influence) concerns at portfolio company level.
- DCSA mitigation negotiations can take **12 months or longer** and may require structural changes to board and ownership register.
- **Impact:** Forecloses classified contracts entirely for non-compliant cap tables; creates threshold conditions that determine which programs a company can bid on and which investors can hold equity.

### Severity 3 — HIGH: Private Equity Exit Pathway Gap

- PE has "largely stayed on the sidelines" in defense, putting early-state investment growth at risk.
- For every $1 the government committed to new-entrant production funding, private investors put in $7 — a 7:1 ratio that risks stalling technology before deployment.
- No fit-for-purpose financing model to carry innovative platforms from prototype to scaled production.
- **Impact:** Critical exit path for venture-backed defense startups is underdeveloped; promising technologies stall before reaching frontline forces.

### Severity 4 — HIGH: Procurement Friction & Timeline Mismatch

- Traditional defense procurement cycles are measured in years; startups operate on months.
- Ukraine's Brave1 model delivers equipment to frontlines in **7 days** vs. months/years in traditional U.S. procurement.
- U.S. system operates without top-down coordination — redundant platforms, fragmented vendor guidance, funding not reaching tactical level.
- **Impact:** Startups cannot wait 2 years for contracts; dual-use companies need civilian market as fallback. Procurement friction kills companies that would otherwise be viable acquisition targets.

### Severity 5 — MODERATE: Classification & Security Clearance Barriers

- Brave1's accessibility depends on "deliberately permissive classification posture" — structurally incompatible with current U.S. classification practices.
- Classified facilities and clearances are acquisition assets (SciTec) but also create barriers to entry.
- **Impact:** Limits the pool of potential acquirers and investors; creates information asymmetry in due diligence.

### Severity 6 — MODERATE: Financing Gap for Production Scale-Up

- Ukraine: 60% of manufacturing capacity sat idle in 2025 for want of contracts — financing is the limiting factor, not industrial capacity.
- U.S. defense VC has grown 10x since 2019 but capital is concentrated at earliest stage; factories, materials, and manufacturing capacity remain constrained.
- **Impact:** Companies can prototype but cannot scale production, reducing their value as acquisition targets and creating supply chain vulnerabilities.

### Severity 7 — LOW: Antitrust / FTC Intervention

- Lockheed Martin's $4.4B acquisition of Aerojet Rocketdyne was blocked by FTC over competitor reliance concerns.
- **Impact:** Rare but consequential; limits vertical integration in critical subsystem markets (propulsion, etc.).

## 5. NP-Hard Problems in Dual-Use Technology & Defense Acquisition

### 5.1 Project Interdiction Problem (Weapons Development Delay)

**Problem:** A project manager (defender) wishes to complete a weapons development program as quickly as possible. An interdictor (adversary) with a limited budget wishes to delay the project's completion by interdicting component tasks.

**Formal Model:** PERT-based interdiction with activity-on-arc (AOA) project networks, formulated as bilevel integer-linear programs.

**Complexity Results:**

| Variant | Complexity | Notes |
|---------|-----------|-------|
| MAXMIN0 (most general: multiple technologies + crashing) | **NP-hard** (may not be in NP) | Evaluating objective requires solving NP-complete ILP |
| DCPMd (decision CPM with alternative technologies) | **Strongly NP-complete** | Reduction from VERTEXCOVERd |
| Single interdiction resource, no crashing | **Polynomial** or pseudo-polynomial | Solvable by simpler methods |
| Fixed number of alternative technologies | **Polynomial** (enumerate combinations) | Only a few ILP instances needed |
| MAXMAX2 (single technology, no crashing) | **Polynomial** / ILP | Network optimization or dynamic program |

**Practical Implication:** In practice, the number of alternative technologies is often small (e.g., 3 mutually exclusive options), making enumeration feasible despite worst-case NP-hardness. The complexity derives from binary variables modeling alternative technologies.

**Source:** Brown, Carlyle, Royset, Wood (2005), Naval Postgraduate School.

### 5.2 Partition Matroid Interdiction Problem (PMI)

**Problem:** A leader (acquirer/interdictor) and follower (defender/project manager) each select independent sets from distinct partition matroids with a common weighted ground set, subject to multiple independent capacity constraints, engaging in a zero-sum game.

**Complexity Results:**

| Scenario | Complexity | Solution Approach |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|
| Both leader and follower groups non-fixed | **Strongly NP-hard** (even with binary weights) | Reduction from Maximum Independent Set |
| Follower groups (k_f) fixed | **Polynomial** O(n^(k_f+1)) | Single-level dual reformulation + greedy |
| Leader groups (k_l) fixed | **Polynomial** O(n^(k_l+1)) | Dynamic programming-based |
| k_f = 1 (single capacity constraint) | **Polynomial** | Greedy algorithm exact |
| k_f ≥ 2 | No constant-factor approximation | Greedy fails |

**Key Insight:** The problem is NOT Σ₂p-hard (unlike knapsack interdiction) and admits a single-level ILP reformulation of polynomial size. Submodularity and monotonicity enable greedy approaches for special cases.

**Source:** arXiv:2401.12010.

### 5.3 Dual-Use Technology Classification Problem

**Problem:** Determining whether a given technology/item falls under ITAR (USML) or EAR (Commerce Control List) — or both — and whether an acquisition triggers FOCI/national security review.

**Complexity Characteristics:**
- **Combinatorial classification:** Technologies can have multiple applications (civilian + military + intelligence), creating a multi-label classification problem.
- **Regulatory arbitrage:** Adversarial states acquire controlled technologies through civilian shell companies, making the classification problem adversarial.
- **Dynamic scope:** Control lists are updated (e.g., EU 2025 update introduced new "500" series classification numbers for computers), requiring continuous re-classification.
- **No known polynomial-time algorithm** for optimal classification across all jurisdictions and regimes (Wassenaar, Australia Group, MTCR, Nuclear Suppliers Group, EU Dual-Use Regulation).

### 5.4 Acquisition Target Selection as Optimization

**Problem:** Given a set of N potential defense-tech targets, each with valuation v_i, capability fit score c_i, regulatory risk r_i, and interdependency constraints, select the optimal portfolio of acquisitions subject to budget B and risk tolerance R.

**Complexity:** This is a **multi-dimensional knapsack problem with dependencies**, which is strongly NP-hard. The interdependency constraints (e.g., acquiring propulsion requires acquiring materials supplier) make it at least as hard as the standard knapsack problem.

**Practical Approaches:**
- Greedy heuristics with capability-fit scoring
- Integer linear programming for small N (< 100 targets)
- Simulation-based approaches for large N with uncertainty
