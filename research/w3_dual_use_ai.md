| **BIS Export Controls** | Dual-use items | Export licensing for dual-use technologies |

### European Union

| Regulation/Policy | Scope | Key Provisions |
|-------------------|-------|----------------|
| **EU Dual-Use Regulation (2021/821)** | Dual-use items including AI | Annex I controls aligned with Wassenaar; Article 5 catch-all for cyber-surveillance items; end-use due diligence required |
| **EU AI Act** | General-purpose AI | GPAI systemic risk provisions (Chapter V); prohibited practices enabling mass surveillance |

### Regulatory Trends

- **Catch-all provisions** expanding: AI systems "designed for or capable of enabling surveillance, interception, or intrusion" may trigger export authorization even if not specifically listed
- **Human control requirements** under debate: Trump administration directed revision of DoD 3000.09 to remove "unnecessary barriers" to rapid autonomous system deployment
- **AI-specific deal representations** becoming standard in M&A transactions
- **Technology transfer offices** cautiously adopting AI tools; barriers include IP leakage risk, lack of transparency, and trust concerns

## 5. Bottlenecks

### Technical Bottlenecks

1. **AI Model Commoditization**
   - Models increasingly commoditized; techniques widely published
   - Reverse engineering often possible
   - Defensibility requires trade secrets, proprietary data, and novel inventions

2. **Adversarial Vulnerabilities**
   - Adversarial AI (A2I) and adversarial ML (AML) attacks can deceive and manipulate models
   - Data poisoning at collection stage has cascading effects on downstream pipeline
   - Physical adversarial attacks on surveillance systems (visible-infrared evasion)

3. **Certification & Assurance**
   - AI systems cannot be fielded due to unresolved certification, accountability, and data reliability questions
   - UK national AI assurance lab estimated at $20M+ investment
   - Lack of predictability and brittleness in defense environments

4. **Data Scarcity & Quality**
   - Defense-specific training data limited and often classified
   - Synthetic data generation required but may not capture real-world complexity
   - Data reliability concerns for compliance with JSP 936 (UK)

### Transactional Bottlenecks

5. **Due Diligence Complexity**
   - AI-specific diligence workstreams required: ownership, training data exclusivity, third-party dependencies, regulatory compliance
   - Most AI products weakly defensible from pure IP standpoint
   - Detailed AI governance records now necessary for transactional readiness

6. **Technology Transfer Friction**
   - IP leakage risk when using third-party AI platforms
   - Lack of transparency in AI-generated outputs
   - Validating AI-based work still requires human experts (patent agents, lawyers)
   - 45% of AI companies located in US, creating concentration risk

7. **Talent & Expertise Gap**
   - Shortage of qualified engineers with defense AI expertise
   - Competition between defense primes and tech companies for AI talent
   - Technology transfer offices lack structured guidance for responsible AI integration

## 6. NP-Hard Problems in AI Surveillance

### Computational Complexity of Surveillance

Several fundamental problems in AI surveillance and autonomous systems are NP-hard, creating inherent scalability and optimization challenges:

| Problem | Complexity | Application | Source |
|---------|-----------|-------------|--------|
| **Art Gallery Problem (AGP)** | NP-hard | Minimum guards to observe entire polygon; sensor placement | O'Rourke 1987; arXiv:2606.21604 |
| **Vertex-Guard AGP** | NP-hard | Guards restricted to polygon vertices; surveillance camera placement | arXiv:2606.21604 |
| **Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA)** | NP-hard | Allocating tracking tasks across robot teams | arXiv:1807.08350 |
| **Minimum Guard Speed for Persistent Tracking** | NP-hard | Finding minimum speed to persistently track mobile intruder | arXiv:1807.08350 |
| **Neural Combinatorial Optimization** | NP-hard (learned) | Training neural policies for guard placement without geometric oracle | arXiv:2606.21604 |

### Implications for AI Surveillance Systems

1. **Scalability Limits:** As surveillance environments grow (more cameras, larger areas, more targets), optimal guard/sensor placement becomes computationally intractable
2. **Approximation Required:** Real-world systems must rely on greedy heuristics, approximation algorithms, or learned policies that provide no optimality guarantees
3. **Neural Approaches:** Reinforcement learning policies (e.g., pointer networks) can construct strong solutions but leave tails of under-covered polygons, particularly out-of-distribution
4. **Adversarial Exploitation:** Physical adversarial attacks exploit the gap between learned policies and optimal solutions — attackers can find evasion strategies that the NP-hard nature of the problem makes difficult to defend against

### Research Frontiers

- **Geo-free neural policies:** Training guard placement without visibility computation, relying on learned geometric representations
- **Encoder probing:** Reading frozen encoder embeddings to understand what neural combinatorial solvers have internalized
- **Physical adversarial attacks:** Understanding temporal persistence, sensing modality, and system-level objectives in surveillance evasion
