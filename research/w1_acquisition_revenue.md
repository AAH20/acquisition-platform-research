## 1. Revenue Model

Acquisition.com operates a **media-driven distribution funnel** that converts attention into revenue through a tiered value ladder, then redeploys that capital into acquisitions, real estate, and venture investments — creating a self-reinforcing flywheel.

### Revenue Streams (Ranked by Contribution)

| Stream | Description | Estimated Price Point |
|--------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Advisory Practice** | Tiered consulting for business owners ($500K–$50M revenue) | L1: ~$5,000 · L2: ~$35,000 · L3: ~$135,000 |
| **Free Content / Media** | YouTube, podcasts, books, newsletters — customer development investment | Free (monetizes over 3–5 year horizon) |
| **Low-Ticket Products** | Books ($100M Offers, $100M Leads), $9/month School membership | $9–$30 |
| **ACQ Network Membership** | Planned B2B membership for vendor negotiating leverage | ~$10,000/yr |
| **PE / Venture Investments** | Majority acquisitions + ACQ Ventures minority checks | $50K–$500K+ per deal |
| **Ancillary Services** | Insurance, lending, sales AI, real estate deals (ACQRE) | Varies |

### The Flywheel Mechanism

```
Free Content → Audience Scale → Advisory Sales → Capital Deployment
      ↑                                                    ↓
      ←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←
```

1. **Attention is the raw input** — free YouTube/podcast content builds massive audience
2. **Sub-$500K businesses** are customer development investments (monetization clock starts at $500K revenue)
3. **Advisory practice** is the primary revenue engine, sold via phone sales team
4. **Capital is redeployed** into acquisitions (51–100% ownership), real estate, and ACQ Ventures
5. **Portfolio success** generates case studies → more free content → more audience

## 3. Unit Economics

### Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) Model

Acquisition.com's CAC is **unconventional** — free content is treated as a long-term customer development cost, not a marketing expense.

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Free Content Investment** | YouTube production, podcast, books — amortized over 3–5 years |
| **Effective CAC** | Near-zero for customers who convert after consuming free content |
| **Paid Acquisition** | Minimal; primarily organic/word-of-mouth |
| **Payback Period** | <90 days for advisory clients (per Hormozi's screening criteria) |

### Lifetime Value (LTV) Framework

| Tier | Price | Expected LTV:CAC |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| L1 Advisory | $5,000 | >3:1 (screening threshold) |
| L2 Advisory | $35,000 | Significantly above 3:1 |
| L3 Advisory | $135,000 | Highest ratio — phone sales self-selects for seriousness |
| ACQ Network | $10,000/yr | Recurring; builds vendor leverage |

### Unit Economics Screening (Hormozi's Framework)

> "How much does it cost to buy a dollar of revenue, and how fast does that dollar come back?"

- **LTV:CAC > 3:1** — minimum threshold for acquisition targets
- **Payback < 90 days** — maximum acceptable payback period
- **Contribution Margin** — must be clearly positive at the atomic unit level
- **Churn Rate** — critical for valuation; high churn compresses multiples

## 5. Monetization Strategy

### The Value Ladder

```
Free Content (YouTube, Podcasts)
    ↓
Low-Ticket ($9/mo School, ~$30 Books)
    ↓
L1 Advisory (~$5,000)
    ↓
L2 Advisory (~$35,000)
    ↓
L3 Advisory (~$135,000)
    ↓
ACQ Network (~$10,000/yr, planned)
    ↓
PE / Venture Investments
```

### Key Monetization Principles

1. **Free content is a 3–5 year CAC** — not charity, but customer development investment
2. **Phone sales for high-ticket** — lets price self-select for seriousness before sales team engages
3. **Membership flips the model** — ACQ Network isn't about dues revenue; it's about vendor negotiating leverage at scale
4. **Deal flow inversion** — audience needs dictate which companies get acquired (not the other way around)
5. **The "onion" model** — every free touchpoint exists to push business owners toward a $135K conversation

## 7. NP-Hard Problems

### 7.1 Optimal Capital Allocation Across Portfolio

**Problem:** Given N acquisition targets with varying EBITDA, growth rates, risk profiles, and capital requirements, determine the optimal allocation of limited capital to maximize portfolio IRR.

**Why NP-Hard:** This is a variant of the **knapsack problem** with interdependent constraints (synergies between acquisitions, operational capacity limits, market timing). The search space grows exponentially with portfolio size.

**Current Approach:** Heuristic-based — Hormozi's screening criteria (LTV:CAC > 3:1, payback < 90 days, $3M+ EBITDA) act as a filter, but optimal allocation remains judgment-driven.

### 7.2 Content-to-Revenue Attribution

**Problem:** Determine which free content pieces (videos, podcasts, books) ultimately drive which advisory sales, given a 3–5 year conversion lag and multiple touchpoints.

**Why NP-Hard:** This is a **multi-touch attribution problem** with delayed feedback. The combinatorial explosion of possible attribution paths makes exact attribution computationally intractable at scale.

**Current Approach:** Proxy metrics — audience size, engagement rates, and self-reported conversion surveys. No rigorous multi-touch attribution model is publicly disclosed.

### 7.3 Acquisition Valuation Under Uncertainty

**Problem:** Value an acquisition target with uncertain future cash flows, variable churn, and unknown operational improvement potential.

**Why NP-Hard:** Requires solving a **stochastic optimization problem** with incomplete information. The posterior distribution of possible outcomes is itself computationally expensive to estimate.

**Current Approach:** Heuristic multiples (3.6x EBITDA for SaaS at 20% YoY growth) + earn-out structures to share risk.

### 7.4 Scaling the Advisory Funnel

**Problem:** Optimize the conversion funnel from free content → low-ticket → L1 → L2 → L3, given finite sales team capacity and varying customer readiness.

**Why NP-Hard:** This is a **dynamic resource allocation problem** with stochastic arrival times and conversion probabilities. The optimal policy depends on the full state of the pipeline.

**Current Approach:** Tiered pricing with phone sales for high-ticket. Self-selection mechanism (price filters for seriousness) reduces optimization burden.
