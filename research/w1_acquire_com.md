## Executive Summary

Acquire.com (founded 2020 as MicroAcquire, rebranded January 2023) is the largest online marketplace for buying and selling profitable online businesses, with a focus on SaaS, ecommerce, and digital service businesses. Founded by Andrew Gazdecki, the platform has facilitated $500M+ in closed deal volume across 2,000+ acquisitions, with 500,000+ registered entrepreneurs and $2B+ in verified buyer funds. The company raised $2.8M in seed funding in 2021 from Bessemer Venture Partners, Naval Ravikant, and Remote First Capital, with total disclosed funding of $6.4M. Headquartered in San Mateo, California, with approximately 40 employees.

## 1. Business Model

### 1.1 Core Model

Acquire.com operates a **two-sided digital marketplace** connecting founders selling online businesses with verified buyers (operators, search funders, holding companies, individuals). The platform uses a **direct founder-to-buyer model** — no traditional broker sits between parties. Gazdecki describes it as "closer to Zillow than to a traditional broker."

**Key characteristics:**
- Sellers list businesses via a structured submission process
- Buyers browse, sign NDAs, request deal rooms, and close transactions through built-in escrow
- The platform provides infrastructure (listings, messaging, NDA flow, escrow integration, document templates) but parties negotiate directly
- Revenue is generated from both sides: sellers pay listing + closing fees; buyers pay subscriptions for premium access

### 1.2 Revenue Streams

| Revenue Stream | Description |
|---|---|
| Seller listing fees | Monthly subscription ($25–$100/mo based on asking price) |
| Seller closing fees | 6–8% of deal value, payable only on successful sale |
| Buyer subscriptions | $0 (Basic) to $780/year (Platinum) |
| Optional premium services | Featured placement, AI diligence tools, managed deals |

## 3. Vetting Process

### 3.1 Seller Vetting

Acquire.com employs **light vetting** compared to full-service brokers:

| Dimension | Acquire.com | Empire Flippers |
|---|---|---|
| Rejection rate | ~55% (approves ~45%) | ~91% |
| Minimum valuation | No hard minimum | ~$100K |
| Revenue requirement | Preferred, not required | $2K/month floor |
| Revenue history | Flexible | 12+ months consistent |
| Review timeline | Days | 3–4 weeks |
| Financial data connection | Optional | Direct system access (Stripe, GA) |
| Category preference | SaaS/tech-first | Content, FBA, mature SaaS, ecommerce |

**What vetting involves:**
- Structured submission process with business details (revenue, growth metrics, tech stack, team size, reason for selling)
- Platform team reviews before listing goes live
- Public teaser shows category, geography, business model, asking price band, high-level metrics
- No proprietary information, domain, or customer data in public teaser

### 3.2 Buyer Vetting

- **Every buyer is verified for funds** — the platform reports $2B+ in confirmed buyer capital
- Buyers must subscribe (Premium or Platinum) to access private financials and chat with founders
- Funds Verification Tool integrated into platform
- Automated NDA required before accessing detailed metrics

### 3.3 Listing Verification Gap

A critical distinction: **listings are seller-reported**. The platform curates and facilitates but does not independently audit each listing's revenue against live source data. Per Deal Alert AI's analysis of 8,000+ listings, approximately **31% of Acquire.com listings showed financial claims inconsistent with supporting documentation** upon buyer investigation. This is substantially higher than Empire Flippers' estimated 8% but lower than unvetted platforms like BizBuySell (~47%).

**Red flags identified:**
- Revenue spiked sharply in last 30–60 days before listing
- Traffic almost entirely "direct" with no believable acquisition source
- Reluctance to grant live, read-only access to billing/analytics
- Metrics shown only as screenshots, never source-connected

## 4. Valuation Methodology

### 4.1 Platform Valuation Tools

Acquire.com provides a **free market-driven valuation** based on hundreds of closed acquisitions, updated quarterly. The platform recommends:

1. **Multiple methodology** (most common): Apply a multiple to SDE, EBITDA, or revenue
   - SDE = (Revenue – Operating Expenses – Cost of Goods) + Owner's Compensation
   - EBITDA for medium/larger businesses
   - Revenue multiple if SDE/EBITDA is zero (growth-stage)
2. **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF):** For startups with predictable cash flows
3. **Cost-to-Duplicate:** For pre-revenue startups; doesn't account for growth potential or intangibles
4. **Berkus Method:** Qualitative valuation based on risk factors; for pre-revenue/seed stage
5. **Risk Factor Summation:** Adjusts baseline valuation by risk factors

### 4.2 Market Multiples (2025–2026 Data)

## 5. Seller Experience

### 5.1 Listing Process

1. **Public teaser:** Category, geography, business model, asking price band, high-level metrics (MRR/revenue range, profit margin, team size, age)
2. **NDA-gated detail:** Paid buyers sign automated NDA → see detailed metrics, business name/URL, customer count, churn, traffic sources, seller's narrative
3. **Buyer-seller chat:** Direct communication in deal room; most deal rooms see 3–10 serious buyer conversations before an offer
4. **LOI and APA:** Platform provides templates for letter of intent and asset purchase agreement
5. **Escrow and close:** Escrow.com default; buyer wires funds, seller transfers assets, escrow releases

### 5.2 Seller Support

- In-house M&A advisors (free)
- Listing optimization
- Marketing campaigns to verified buyers (social media, newsletters to 500k+ buyers)
- Introductions to pro buyers (PE, VIPs)
- Unlimited access to Acquire Academy
- Free escrow through Escrow.com
- Guidance through valuation, due diligence, escrow

### 5.4 Common Seller Frustrations

- **NDA harvesting:** "I got 15 NDAs and zero serious offers" — buyers sign NDAs to research market, not to buy

- **Lowball offers:** Many initial offers at 30–50% below asking
- **Stale listings:** If business doesn't match active buyer searches, listing can sit indefinitely
- **Closing fee impact:** On small deals, the percentage fee stings ($2,500 on a $50K deal)

## 6. Buyer Experience

### 6.1 Buyer Process

1. **Browse and filter:** By revenue model, MRR range, asking multiple, niche, tech stack
2. **Send qualified introduction:** Credibility in first message is worth more than higher offer in third
3. **Sign NDA and get data room:** P&L statements, MRR dashboard, churn data, tech stack details
4. **Verify at source:** Read-only access to Stripe/Paddle/Braintree; match MRR claims against live data
5. **Schedule founder call:** Deep dive on operations, technical debt, growth levers
6. **Submit LOI:** Non-binding; outlines offer price, payment structure, transition period
7. **Legal, escrow, transfer:** Hire deal attorney; set up escrow; transfer codebase, domain, payment processor, customer list
8. **Negotiate transition period:** Standard 30–90 days seller support

### 6.2 Buyer Advantages

- **Speed:** Direct deals can close in 3–6 weeks (vs. 8–16 weeks with brokers); some in 22 days
- **No broker commission:** Theoretically more room in negotiation
- **Direct founder access:** Unfiltered technical and operational information
- **Structural flexibility:** Sellers open to creative deals (seller financing, earnouts, partial acqui-hire)
- **Deep SaaS inventory:** Largest concentration of micro-SaaS deal flow anywhere

### 6.3 Buyer Challenges

- **No mandatory verification:** Buyer bears full due diligence burden
- **Quality variance:** Listings range from legitimate 7-figure businesses to hobby projects
- **Aspirational pricing:** Many listings at multiples no rational buyer would pay
- **Technical DD required:** SaaS due diligence requires technical knowledge (code review, infrastructure, dependencies)
- **No broker safety net:** If migration goes sideways or seller stops responding, no account manager to fix it
- **Customer concentration risk:** Businesses with 40%+ revenue from single customer are extremely risky

### 6.4 Buyer Due Diligence Checklist (Recommended)

1. Trace revenue to source (read-only payment processor access, 24 months data)
2. Separate recurring from non-recurring revenue
3. Run customer concentration analysis (top 1, 5, 10 accounts)
4. Pull real churn by cohort (18–24 months)
5. Audit traffic and acquisition channels
6. Commission independent technical code review ($500–$1,500)
7. Map every third-party dependency
8. Verify ownership of all assets (domain, code, IP)
9. Calculate true owner earnings (replace founder labor at market rates)
10. Define transition in writing (hours, period, scope)

## 9. NP-Hard Problems in Acquire.com's Operations

While the web search for "acquire.com NP-hard problems" returned general computational complexity theory rather than platform-specific analysis, several of Acquire.com's core operational challenges map to well-known NP-hard problems:

### 9.1 Optimal Buyer-Seller Matching (Bipartite Matching with Constraints)

**Problem:** Given N sellers and M buyers with heterogeneous preferences (budget constraints, category preferences, growth requirements, timing, risk tolerance), find the assignment that maximizes total match quality or transaction probability.

**Complexity:** Basic bipartite matching is polynomial (Hungarian algorithm, O(n³)). However, with multi-dimensional constraints (budget, category, timing, portfolio synergies), the problem becomes a **multi-dimensional assignment problem** or **generalized assignment problem (GAP)**, which is NP-hard.

**Acquire.com relevance:** The platform's AI-driven predictive matching (pilot tests showed 22% lift in engaged leads, 12% drop in time-to-offer) attempts to solve this heuristically. Optimal matching could increase deal velocity by ~30%.

### 9.2 Portfolio Optimization for Buyers (Knapsack Variant)

**Problem:** A buyer with a fixed budget wants to acquire a portfolio of businesses that maximizes some objective (revenue, diversification, synergy) subject to budget, operational capacity, and risk constraints.

**Complexity:** This is a variant of the **0/1 Knapsack problem** (NP-hard) or **Multi-Objective Knapsack** (strongly NP-hard). With synergies between items (complementary businesses), it becomes a **Quadratic Knapsack Problem**, which is NP-hard.

**Acquire.com relevance:** Search funders and holding companies using Acquire.com acquire multiple businesses. Optimal portfolio selection would maximize returns on limited capital.

### 9.3 Due Diligence Scheduling (Job Shop Scheduling)

**Problem:** Coordinate multiple due diligence processes across buyers, sellers, legal advisors, financial auditors, and technical reviewers, each with different availability and sequencing requirements, to minimize total time-to-close.

**Complexity:** **Job Shop Scheduling** is NP-hard. Even the simplified "3 jobs × 3 machines" instance can take meaningful compute time to solve exactly.

**Acquire.com relevance:** The platform's 30–60 day close window depends on efficient scheduling of diligence tasks. Bottlenecks in scheduling directly impact deal velocity.

### 9.4 Optimal Auction/Market Design (Mechanism Design)

**Problem:** Design auction rules or market mechanisms that maximize platform revenue, seller outcomes, and buyer satisfaction simultaneously.

**Complexity:** Optimal mechanism design often involves solving **winner determination problems** in combinatorial auctions, which are NP-hard (reducible to Weighted Set Packing).

**Acquire.com relevance:** The platform's passive marketplace model vs. auction models (like ExitBid's 5-day auctions) represents different mechanism design choices. Optimal design could address the "no urgency/competition" bottleneck.

### 9.5 Valuation Optimization (Combinatorial Optimization)

**Problem:** Determine optimal asking prices for listings considering market conditions, comparable deals, buyer behavior, and platform fee structures to maximize expected platform revenue.

**Complexity:** With interdependent listings (buyers compare across listings), this becomes a **combinatorial optimization problem** with potential NP-hardness.

**Acquire.com relevance:** The platform's valuation database (1,200+ closed deals) and biannual multiples reports provide data, but optimal pricing across the marketplace remains computationally challenging.

### 9.6 Practical Implications

Acquire.com addresses these NP-hard problems through:
- **Heuristics and ML:** AI-driven matching (22% lift in engaged leads)
- **Simplified workflows:** Standardized NDA/LOI/APA templates reduce complexity
- **Human-in-the-loop:** M&A advisors handle complex cases
- **Market mechanisms:** Escrow-first design reduces trust/verification complexity
