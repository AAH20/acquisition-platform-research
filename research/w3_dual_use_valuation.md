# W3: Dual-Use Technology Valuation Methods

**Research Focus:** Valuation methods for dual-use technology companies (companies serving both commercial and defense/government markets).

**Date:** 2026-10-05

---

## Executive Summary

Dual-use technology companies—those generating revenue from both commercial and government/defense markets—command a measurable valuation premium over pure-play defense contractors. This premium reflects structural differences in customer concentration, revenue durability, and margin profiles. Traditional defense primes trade at 1.6–2.7x revenue in public markets, while dual-use defense technology companies in private markets command multiples an order of magnitude higher (15–50x forward revenue). The valuation challenge is compounded by extended development cycles, procurement conversion risk, and the absence of robust public comparables for early-stage dual-use companies.

---

## 1. Valuation Methods

### 1.1 Income Approach (Discounted Cash Flow)

The income approach is the most commonly used method for IP and technology valuation. It values an asset based on the expected economic income it will generate, discounted to present value. For dual-use companies, DCF must account for:

- **Contracted forward revenue** (not trailing revenue) as the denominator
- **Recompete win rates** (typically 70–85% for incumbents)
- **New business win rates** (typically 20–40%)
- **Working capital requirements** (government payment cycles)
- **Terminal value** assumptions

**Key DCF considerations for dual-use:**
- Revenue visibility from multi-year government contracts
- Commercial revenue base providing diversification
- Budget cycle risk and political priority shifts
- Prototype-to-production conversion risk

### 1.2 Market Approach (Comparables)

The market approach benchmarks value against comparable transactions or publicly traded companies. For dual-use technology:

- **Public comps:** Traditional defense primes (Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, RTX, General Dynamics) trade at 1.6–2.7x revenue
- **Private comps:** Dual-use defense tech companies command significantly higher multiples
- **Scarcity of comparables:** Early-stage dual-use companies have limited public market analogs

### 1.3 Cost Approach

The cost method estimates value based on resources required to recreate or replace the IP. Most useful for very early-stage assets (TRL 1–4) with limited commercial proof points. Lacks predictive power for future revenues.

### 1.4 Real Options / Optionality Pricing

A three-stage real option framework integrating binomial option pricing with income approach has been applied to defense R&D projects. This method uses stock-return-based volatility and weapon system application rate as key parameters. The "optionality premium" reflects the value of having both commercial and defense market paths.

## 3. DCF (Discounted Cash Flow) Analysis

### 3.1 Defense Tech DCF Considerations

- **WACC:** Typically 7–10% for established defense contractors; higher for early-stage dual-use companies
- **Terminal growth:** 3–5% for mature defense; higher for dual-use with commercial upside
- **Contract backlog:** Must be risk-adjusted (funded backlog at 1.0x, unfunded options at 0.6–0.8x, IDIQ ceilings at 0.1x)
- **Recompete risk:** Major contracts expiring within 12 months reduce valuation

### 3.2 Look-Through DCF (ETF Example)

Global X Defense Tech ETF (SHLD) look-through DCF analysis (2026):
- Fund trades 29% above combined intrinsic value
- Holdings valued using conservative 5-year DCF
- Growth rate = slower of multi-year revenue trend and FCF trend
- 88% coverage (43 of 50 holdings valued)

### 3.3 Reverse DCF

Used to assess whether current valuations are supported by visible fundamentals. Example: Kratos Defense (KTOS) at $69/share requires ~7% WACC + 4.5% terminal growth or a $5B+ revenue runway not yet visible in consensus estimates.

## 5. Valuation Bottlenecks

### 5.1 Structural Bottlenecks

1. **Customer concentration risk:** Pure-play defense contractors depend on government procurement offices operating on multi-year budget cycles subject to political priority shifts
2. **Extended development cycles:** Dual-use companies require longer development timelines, delaying revenue recognition
3. **Prototype-to-production conversion:** Not all prototypes convert to production contracts; this execution risk is embedded in valuations
4. **Export market uncertainty:** International expansion is assumed but not guaranteed
5. **Margin expansion assumptions:** Every multiple bundles assumptions about budget growth, prototype conversion, export markets, and margin expansion at scale

### 5.2 Information Bottlenecks

1. **Revenue quality assessment:** Distinguishing funded backlog from unfunded ceilings and IDIQ "hunting licenses"
2. **Set-aside revenue cliff:** 8(a) and SDVOSB revenue may be legally tethered to seller's small business status, creating a "melting ice cube" valuation problem
3. **Recertification risk:** SBA rule changes (2026) may render acquired companies ineligible for future set-aside contracts
4. **Reputation balance sheet:** Dual-use companies must manage two narratives (commercial + defense), and misalignment can impair valuation

### 5.3 Market Bottlenecks

1. **Scarcity of public comps:** Few publicly traded pure-play dual-use technology companies exist
2. **Private market opacity:** Private company valuations are not subject to market discipline
3. **Denominator ambiguity:** Forward revenue vs. trailing revenue vs. run-rate revenue can produce wildly different multiples
4. **Stage dispersion:** Multiples vary dramatically by funding stage (13.3x at seed to 35.5x at Series D)

## 6. NP-Hard Problems in Dual-Use Valuation

### 6.1 Computational Complexity

Valuing dual-use technology companies involves several NP-hard or computationally intractable problems:

1. **Portfolio optimization:** Selecting optimal R&D project portfolios under budget constraints with uncertain outcomes is a variant of the knapsack problem
2. **Real options valuation:** Pricing compound real options with multiple underlying stochastic processes requires Monte Carlo simulation or approximate dynamic programming
3. **Contract waterfall optimization:** Optimally allocating resources across a portfolio of contracts with uncertain exercise probabilities and interdependent values
4. **Recompete timing:** Determining optimal timing for recompete bids under uncertainty is a stochastic optimal stopping problem

### 6.2 Real-World Implications

- **NP-Hard Ventures:** An Amsterdam-based VC firm (founded 2021) explicitly named after the computational complexity class, backing product-obsessed founders in robotics, autonomous systems, and defense technology
- **Heuristic approaches:** Practical valuation relies on scenario-based forecasting, expert judgment, and simplified models rather than exact optimization
- **Iterative valuation:** As technologies mature (TRL 1–4 → TRL 6–9), valuations transition from qualitative/scenario-driven to quantitative/standardized methods

### 6.3 Research Frontiers

- **Technology readiness level (TRL) progression:** Traditional valuation methods yield overly broad ranges at low TRLs; scenario-based approaches are needed
- **Volatility estimation:** Stock-return-based volatility used as parameter in real option models for defense R&D
- **Weapon system application rate:** Key parameter in defense R&D investment prioritization frameworks

## 9. Key Takeaways

1. **Dual-use premium is structural:** Private capital explicitly prices the advantage of dual-market revenue (commercial + defense) over single-market exposure.
2. **Multiples vary by stage and denominator:** Use median (not average) multiples; clarify whether the denominator is trailing, forward, or run-rate revenue.
3. **Backlog quality matters:** Funded backlog > unfunded options > IDIQ ceilings. Apply risk-adjusted discount rates to each layer.
4. **DCF requires scenario-based approach:** Traditional DCF is insufficient for early-stage dual-use; real options and scenario forecasting are more appropriate.
5. **NP-hard problems abound:** Portfolio optimization, real options pricing, and contract waterfall optimization are computationally intractable; practical valuation relies on heuristics and expert judgment.
6. **Information asymmetry is a major bottleneck:** Revenue quality, set-aside eligibility, and reputation balance sheet are often opaque in dual-use company valuations.
