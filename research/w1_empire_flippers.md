## Executive Summary

Empire Flippers (EF) is the world's largest curated online business marketplace, founded in 2011 by Justin Cooke and Joe Magnotti. It operates as a two-sided brokerage platform connecting vetted sellers of online businesses with pre-qualified buyers. As of 2026, EF has facilitated over $600M in closed deals across 2,665+ businesses, with a 4.4/5 Trustpilot rating and six-time Inc. 5000 honoree status. The company's core differentiator is its rigorous vetting process — rejecting 91% of seller applications — which creates a trust premium that justifies its 15% commission on most deals.

## 1. Business Model

### 1.1 Core Model
Empire Flippers operates as a **full-service curated marketplace and brokerage** for online businesses. The platform handles the entire transaction lifecycle end-to-end:

- **Seller side:** Application → Vetting → Valuation → Listing → Marketing → Negotiation → Escrow → Migration
- **Buyer side:** Registration → Identity/Proof-of-Funds Verification → Listing Unlock → Due Diligence → Offer → Wire Transfer → Migration

### 1.2 Revenue Streams
| Stream | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Seller Commission (Primary)** | Marginal/blended commission on sale price: $10K flat under $66,667; 15% up to $700K; 8% on $700K–$5M; 2.5% above $5M |
| **Buyer Transaction Fee** | $0–$2,500 processing fee (buyers pay no commission) |
| **Empire Flippers Capital (EF Capital)** | Investment fund arm (rebranded as WebStreet in April 2023, now separated) — accredited investor fund with $25K minimum, 20% projected returns |
| **Affiliate Program** | ~$2,000 per qualified transaction depending on deal size and side |

## 2. Fees

### 2.1 Commission Structure (Marginal/Blended)

| Sale Price Band | Commission Rate | Example |
|-----------------|-----------------|---------|

| Above $5,000,000 | 2.5% (on portion above $5M) | $5M sale → $449K (9.0% effective) |

### 2.2 Fee Comparison with Competitors

| Platform | Listing Fee | Success Fee | Total on $300K Sale | Model |
|----------|-------------|-------------|---------------------|-------|

## 3. Vetting Process

### 3.1 Seller Vetting
EF's vetting is the most selective in the industry:

- **Acceptance rate:** ~5–9% (91% rejection rate based on 6,413 submissions from July 2020–June 2021)
- **Vetting timeline:** 3–4 weeks before listing goes live
- **Listing publishes:** Every Monday

### 3.2 Vetting Criteria
| Requirement | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| Minimum net profit | $1,500–$2,000/month (12-month average) |
| Revenue history | 12+ months minimum |
| Analytics tracking | Google Analytics or Clicky for 3+ months before applying |
| Ad-dependent sites | Must be on same domain for full 12 months |
| Service businesses | 50%+ revenue from multiple clients |
| Financial records | Up to 3 years, or life of business |

### 3.3 Verification Scope
EF verifies:
- ✅ Revenue (Stripe, Shopify, Amazon Seller Central data)
- ✅ Traffic (Google Analytics access)
- ✅ Seller identity (hard verification)
- ❌ Expenses — **NOT independently verified** (per their Terms of Use: "Empire does not independently verify the expenses of the Business")

### 3.4 Buyer Vetting
- Identity verification required
- Proof of funds verification required
- One-time verification unlocks all listings in buyer's price range
- Old 5% refundable deposit system eliminated

### 3.5 Vetting as Competitive Moat
The 91% rejection rate is the single most important fact about EF. It creates:
- A trust premium that justifies 15% fees
- A quality floor that competitors cannot match
- A curated inventory that attracts sophisticated buyers
- A network effect: more quality listings → more buyers → more sellers

## 4. Valuation

### 4.1 Valuation Methodology
EF uses a **multiple-based valuation** approach:

- **Formula:** Listing Price = Average Monthly Net Profit × Multiple
- **Typical range:** 20x–60x monthly net profit (30x–50x most common)
- **Annual equivalent:** ~2.5x–4x+ yearly net profit
- **As of September 2026:** EF switched display to annual multiples (divide monthly by 12)

### 4.2 Valuation by Business Type (2026 Data)
| Category | Monthly Multiple | Annual Multiple |
|----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Distressed businesses | 13.7x | 1.1x |
| Typical businesses | 27.8x | 2.3x |
| Premium businesses | 31.1x | 2.6x |
| 7-figure premium | 41x | 3.4x |

### 4.3 Valuation Factors
- Business model and revenue diversification
- Age and track record
- Growth trajectory (declining revenue is a negotiating chip, not disqualifier)
- Traffic concentration and keyword defensibility
- Supplier/customer concentration
- Owner dependency
- Tech stack maintenance burden

### 4.4 Valuation Philosophy
- Recurring revenue commands higher multiples (subscriptions, memberships)
- Systematized businesses with documented processes get premium valuations
- Buyers pay for predictable cash flow and scalability
- EF's valuation tool API provides automated valuations based on real closed deals

## 6. Buyer Experience

### 6.1 Buyer Journey
1. **Register** — Create marketplace account
2. **Verify** — Identity + proof of funds (one-time)
3. **Search & Unlock** — Browse listings, unlock to see URL and full financials
4. **Due Diligence** — Review financials, traffic, operations; Q&A with seller
5. **Make Offer** — Submit offer with due diligence option
6. **Circulation Period** — 24-hour counteroffer window if offer < list price
7. **Wire Transfer** — First wire wins ("wire race")
8. **Migration** — EF team coordinates asset transfer (3–8 weeks)

### 6.2 Buyer Value Propositions
- **Verified financials:** 100% of listings have revenue independently verified
- **Quality floor:** Highest average deal quality score (7.2/10) among major platforms
- **Speed:** Fastest average close time (47 days)
- **Migration support:** In-house team handles technical transfer
- **Curated inventory:** No junk listings or scams
- **Structured process:** Clear buy-it-now and wire race mechanics

### 6.3 Buyer Pain Points
- **Low volume:** Only 60–80 new listings per month
- **Fast competition:** Best deals go under offer within 24–72 hours
- **Less negotiation room:** Only 6.1% average discount from ask
- **Unlock requirement:** Must verify identity/funds before seeing full details
- **Wire race pressure:** Must move decisively on hot listings

## 9. NP-Hard Problems in Empire Flippers' Operations

### 9.1 Computational Complexity of Marketplace Operations

While Empire Flippers does not explicitly frame its challenges in computational complexity theory, several of its core operational problems map to known NP-hard problem classes:

### 9.2 Buyer-Seller Matching (Assignment Problem Variant)
**Problem:** Match buyers to listings based on preferences, capital constraints, business model fit, and timing.

**Complexity:** The general assignment problem is NP-hard. With thousands of buyers and hundreds of listings, each with multi-dimensional preferences (price range, business model, risk tolerance, growth expectations), finding the optimal matching is computationally intractable.

**EF's Approach:** AI-enhanced matching analyzing thousands of historic CRM notes to surface relevant opportunities — a heuristic approach to an NP-hard problem.

### 9.3 Valuation Optimization (Multi-Objective Optimization)
**Problem:** Determine optimal listing price considering multiple competing objectives: maximize sale price, minimize time to sale, maximize probability of closing.

**Complexity:** Multi-objective optimization with conflicting objectives is NP-hard. The valuation must account for revenue stability, growth trajectory, market conditions, buyer psychology, and competitive dynamics simultaneously.

**EF's Approach:** Proprietary valuation framework using historical closed deal data — essentially a learned heuristic.

### 9.4 Portfolio Selection for EF Capital (Knapsack Variant)
**Problem:** Select portfolio of businesses for EF Capital fund given capital constraints, diversification requirements, and return targets.

**Complexity:** The knapsack problem and its variants are NP-hard. Selecting optimal portfolio of businesses under multiple constraints (sector diversification, risk limits, capital deployment) is computationally hard.

### 9.5 Scheduling and Resource Allocation
**Problem:** Schedule vetting reviews, listing publications, migration team assignments, and buyer-seller calls across limited staff resources.

**Complexity:** Job-shop scheduling and resource-constrained project scheduling are NP-hard problems.

### 9.6 Due Diligence Verification (Constraint Satisfaction)
**Problem:** Verify that a business's financial claims are consistent across multiple data sources (Stripe, Google Analytics, tax filings, ad networks).

**Complexity:** Constraint satisfaction problems (CSP) are NP-hard in general. Cross-referencing multiple data sources for consistency is a CSP.

### 9.7 Why This Matters
Understanding these problems as NP-hard explains why EF relies on:
- **Heuristics and human expertise** rather than optimal algorithms
- **AI/ML models** trained on historical data for matching and valuation
- **Structured processes** that reduce problem complexity through standardization
- **Human-in-the-loop** decision making for complex edge cases
