**Research Date:** 2026-10-05  
**Focus:** Dual-use human performance and training technology M&A landscape

## Market Overview

The defense training and simulation M&A market is experiencing a significant upswing. Capstone Partners reported a **47.5% increase in training and simulation deal volume in 2025**, with 14 additional deals announced or completed in early 2026 (up 133.3% year-over-year). Strategic buyers dominated, comprising **72.9% of deals** in 2025 with volume up 79.2% to 43 transactions.

**Key transactions:**
- **CAE / L3Harris Military Training** — $1.05B (~13.5x estimated adjusted EBITDA, ~2.1x revenue)
- **BAE Systems / Bohemia Interactive Simulations** — $200M
- **Veritas Capital & Evergreen Coast Capital / Cubic** — ~$3.0B take-private (including debt)
- **Shield AI / Aechelon Technology** — immersive visualization
- **By Light (Sagewind) / Dignitas Technologies** — modeling, simulation & training
- **Greenbriar Equity / Arotech** — training and simulation division

**Demand drivers:**
- NATO allies increasing defense spending toward 5% threshold
- Military adoption of VR, AR, and AI for combat scenario replication
- Civilian segment demand for streamlined mission-critical training
- PE re-engagement in 2026 after two flat years (six PE deals YTD, five add-ons)

**Buyer priorities:** Program positions on long-running training contracts, proprietary simulation software/visual databases, recurring revenue from sustainment and software licenses, cleared engineers, and multi-service customer bases.

## Valuation

**Revenue multiples observed:**
- Proprietary simulation software with program positions: **~2.1x revenue** (CAE/L3Harris)
- Training services (labor-heavy): **~0.36x revenue** (Cubic training unit)
- EBITDA multiples for premium assets: **~13.5x adjusted EBITDA**

**Valuation drivers:**
1. **Backlog depth** — CAE's Defense & Security segment: C$10.8B adjusted backlog, 1.10x trailing book-to-sales
2. **Proprietary software vs. instructor labor** — the main valuation divide
3. **Program positions** — seats on named aircrew/weapons/synthetic training programs with years of remaining period of performance
4. **Recurring revenue mix** — device sustainment, instructor support, software licenses
5. **Customer diversification** — across services (Air Force, Navy, Army) or allied militaries

**Premium indicators:** Proprietary simulation software, visual databases, device designs that customers cannot easily re-compete; cleared personnel; multi-year program positions.

## Bottlenecks

### Operational Bottlenecks
- **Training as a performance fix is overused:** Training is identified as the root cause of performance deficiencies only **~12% of the time** (Navy HPC data). Most performance gaps stem from unclear objectives, improper tools, and ineffective processes.
- **Human capital due diligence gap:** No established framework exists for assessing leadership structural capacity. Current behavioral assessments rely on self-report, which is systematically unreliable.
- **Key-person risk:** 81.4% of near-capacity executives misidentify the structural domain of their actual problem (95% CI: 80.7–82.2%), making self-report-based assessment unreliable precisely when accuracy matters most.
- **Integration risk:** 55% of merged companies underperform sector average in share value post-M&A; worst performers are led by acquirer's management alone and lose employees.

### Cognitive Bottlenecks
- **Capacity limits create information bottlenecks:** In rapid motor behaviors, limited-capacity pipelined processing creates bottlenecks. Multiplicative task demands disproportionately affect the best performers.
- **Attention as a shared resource:** Processes competing for limited attention create bottlenecks; learning/automation can reduce resource demands.
- **Structural vs. flexible bottlenecks:** Systems with structural bottlenecks are at the mercy of task demands; flexible resource allocation can mitigate but not eliminate bottlenecks.

## NP-Hard Problems in Human Performance

Human decision-making frequently operates on problems that are computationally intractable (NP-hard), yet humans consistently find near-optimal solutions:

1. **Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP):** Humans efficiently explore the solution space of this NP-complete problem to find near-optimal tours. Performance decays linearly with graph size, and visuo-spatial abilities significantly affect outcomes.

2. **Knapsack & Vertex Cover:** Humans solve NP-hard optimization versions of these problems. Genetic algorithms outperform humans in speed, but human performance degrades gracefully as instance size scales.

3. **Instance complexity predicts human effort:** Structural properties of individual NP-hard problem instances (typical-case complexity, instance complexity) predict both decision quality and time expended. Higher complexity → more time, lower quality.

4. **Implications for training technology:** Human performance on NP-hard tasks depends on problem structure, not just training level. This suggests training technologies should focus on pattern recognition, heuristic development, and cognitive flexibility rather than rote skill acquisition.
