# Wave 1 Research: Acquisition.com Buyer Experience & Deal Discovery

**Date:** 2026-10-04
**Focus:** Buyer-side experience on the Acquire.com (formerly MicroAcquire) marketplace
**Method:** 10 web searches, top 3 results each, synthesized

---

## 1. Buyer Journey

The buyer journey on Acquire.com follows a structured 5-phase progression spanning approximately 6–12 weeks from initial inquiry to deal close:

| Phase | Timeline | Key Activities |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| **1. Discovery & Initial Inquiry** | Weeks 1–2 | Browse public teasers, filter by criteria, express interest via messaging system |
| **2. Initial Conversation & Qualification** | Weeks 2–3 | Direct founder-buyer chat, gauge seriousness, assess founder credibility and buyer intent |
| **3. NDA & Due Diligence** | Weeks 3–6 | Sign automated NDA, access private financials in deal room, conduct deep diligence (financial, operational, technical, legal) |
| **4. LOI & APA Negotiation** | Weeks 6–8 | Submit Letter of Intent via LOI builder, negotiate terms, sign Asset Purchase Agreement using APA builder |
| **5. Escrow & Close** | Weeks 8–12 | Escrow.com verifies buyer funds, asset transfer (domain, code, customer list, contracts), funds release upon approval |

**Key Journey Characteristics:**
- **Direct founder access:** Buyers speak directly with the person who built the business—no broker interpretation layer
- **Pre-motivated parties:** Both sides have already decided to transact, eliminating extended prospecting
- **Standardized tooling:** Platform provides LOI builder, APA builder, data room, and escrow integration
- **Typical close window:** 30–60 days from LOI to funds released for clean deals; 60–120 days with SBA financing

## 2. Discovery Mechanism

Acquire.com employs a **tiered disclosure model** that balances transparency with information protection:

### Public Teaser (All Visitors)
- Category, geography, business model
- Asking price band
- High-level metrics: MRR/revenue range, profit margin, team size, business age
- No proprietary information, domain, or customer data

### NDA-Gated Detail (Premium/Platinum Buyers)
- Detailed metrics: actual business name, URL, customer count, churn, traffic sources
- Seller's narrative on growth potential and risk
- Full financials, SaaS metrics (LTV, CAC, ARR, MRR, churn rate)

### Deal Room
- Private chat between buyer and seller
- 3–10 serious buyer conversations typical before an offer
- Document sharing via built-in data room
- Automated NDA execution

### AI-Assisted Discovery (Platinum Tier)
- AI-powered due diligence checklist
- Startup recommendations based on buyer profile
- Priority support and early access to new listings

### Buyer Subscription Tiers

| Tier | Price | Access Level |
|------|-------|-------------|
| **Basic** | Free | View public listings only; no private details, no chat |
| **Premium** | $390/year | Listings up to $250K TTM revenue; chat with founders; view private financials |
| **Platinum** | $780/year | All listings including managed deals; priority support; AI diligence tools; startup recommendations |

## 3. Search & Filtering

### Available Filters
- **Industry/category** (SaaS, ecommerce, agencies, content sites, mobile apps, service businesses)
- **Revenue range** (MRR/ARR bands)
- **Business model** (subscription, marketplace, ecommerce, service)
- **Growth trajectory**
- **Geography**
- **Asking price band**

### Platform Search Features
- **Favorites:** Save listings for later review
- **Archive:** Remove listings from active view without deleting
- **Hide/Un-hide:** Temporarily remove listings from search results
- **Recommended notifications:** AI-driven alerts for new listings matching buyer criteria
- **Search filters:** Dedicated filter interface for narrowing results

### Discovery Limitations
- **Passive marketplace model:** No auction mechanism or competitive bidding; buyers must proactively search
- **No urgency creation:** Listings can sit indefinitely if they don't match active buyer searches
- **Engagement decay:** Listings that don't stand out in first 1–2 weeks see sharp engagement drop-off
- **Tier-gated access:** Basic (free) buyers cannot view private details or chat with founders, limiting discovery to public teasers only

## 4. Buyer Protection

### Identity & Funds Verification
- **Buyer verification:** Platform checks buyer identity and funds
- **Verified funds badge:** Indicates buyer has capital to acquire ($2B+ in verified buyer capital across platform)
- **Trusted Buyer badge:** Awarded for responsiveness and professionalism; signals serious intent

### Transaction Protection
- **Automated NDA:** Standard NDA executed automatically before sharing sensitive data
- **Data room:** Virtual folder for secure document sharing with access control
- **Escrow.com integration:** Default settlement layer; verifies buyer funds before asset transfer
- **Asset transfer protection:** Buyer inspects assets within agreed inspection period before approving release
- **Conditional payments:** Escrow supports seller holdbacks and earnouts

### Legal Protection
- **LOI builder:** Standardized letter of intent template
- **APA builder:** Asset purchase agreement template with customizable terms
- **M&A lawyer recommendations:** Platform recommends legal counsel for buyers
- **AI-powered due diligence checklist:** Structured diligence framework

### Protection Gaps
- **Minimal financial verification:** ~31% of listings show financial claims inconsistent with supporting documentation (vs. ~8% on Empire Flippers)
- **No broker mediation:** Platform does not mediate disputes; buyers responsible for resolution
- **Non-binding LOIs:** Buyers can walk away at any point during due diligence
- **No white-glove guidance:** Basic support only via email and in-app messaging

## 5. Buyer Financing

### SBA 7(a) Loans (Primary Financing Mechanism)
A large share of deals in the $300K–$5M band close with SBA 7(a) financing:

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Maximum loan size | $5M |
| Down payment | 10% minimum (+ 5% seller standby note common) |
| Term | Up to 10 years (business only); up to 25 years (with real estate) |
| Rate | Prime + 2.25%–2.75% (variable) |
| SBA guarantee | Up to 75% of loan amount |

**Advantages for buyers:**
- Long amortization preserves positive cash flow on day one
- $250K SDE financed over 10 years with $100K down → ~$130K–$145K annual debt service
- Enables ETA (Entrepreneurship Through Acquisition) model

**SBA limitations:**
- Requires 2+ years of clean tax returns and audited financials
- Buyer must operate business full-time post-close (no passive investors)
- Extends closing timelines to 60–120 days (vs. 30–60 for all-cash)
- Some sellers explicitly decline SBA buyers due to longer close and seller note requirements

### Other Financing Mechanisms
- **Seller financing:** Negotiated directly between parties; cannot be transacted through Escrow.com
- **Earnouts:** Conditional payments tied to post-acquisition performance milestones
- **Pre-approved buyers:** Many Platinum-tier buyers come pre-approved from specialized SBA lenders (Live Oak Bank, Wells Fargo)

## 6. Buyer Challenges & Bottlenecks

### Financial Verification Burden
- **31% inconsistency rate:** Nearly 1 in 3 listings show financial claims that don't match documentation
- **Buyer responsibility:** Platform conducts only basic checks; buyers must independently validate MRR, CAC, churn
- **Required evidence:** 12 months of bank statements, payment processor records, accounting software integrations, customer interviews
- **Risk amplification:** First-time buyers face material risk; experienced investors can manage the burden

### Information Asymmetry
- **Inflated revenue claims:** Listings using "potential" or "projected" revenue show 58% higher failure-to-close rates
- **Customer concentration risk:** Businesses with 40%+ revenue from single customer are existential risks (34 tracked deals: 24 serious complications, 7 total failures)
- **Quality variance:** Less curation than boutique platforms; noise-to-signal ratio is higher

### Process Bottlenecks
- **NDA harvesting:** Buyers sign NDAs on multiple listings with no intent to acquire—researching market, not buying
- **LOI ghosting:** Most common complaint—buyers submit LOI then disappear during due diligence
- **Slow timelines:** 3–6 months from listing to close is common; some deals never close
- **Single-buyer negotiation:** No competing bids means no upward price pressure; lowball offers (30–50% below asking) are common
- **No urgency mechanism:** Passive marketplace lacks auction-style competitive dynamics

### Structural Limitations
- **No broker support:** Platform provides templates and tools but no advisory/mediation services
- **Limited buyer pool at premium tiers:** Subscription model filters casual browsers but also limits total buyer pool
- **Category mismatch:** Content sites, early-stage projects, non-tech businesses, and sub-$5K MRR listings struggle to find buyers
- **SBA friction:** Financing contingency introduces lender risk and extends timelines

## 7. NP-Hard Problems in Buyer Experience

### 7.1 Multi-Dimensional Matching Problem
**Problem:** Match buyers to optimal listings across multiple weighted dimensions (industry, revenue, business model, growth, geography, deal structure, risk tolerance).

**Why NP-Hard:** Equivalent to the multidimensional knapsack problem with soft constraints. Each buyer has a utility function across 10+ dimensions; each listing has a profile across the same dimensions. Finding the globally optimal matching is computationally intractable as the marketplace scales.

**Current Approach:** Simple filter-based search + AI recommendations (Platinum tier). No optimization engine; buyers manually browse and self-select.

### 7.2 Trust Verification Problem
**Problem:** Verify financial claims of anonymous sellers without full audit.

**Why NP-Hard:** Requires validating self-reported metrics (MRR, churn, CAC, LTV) against ground truth. Partial verification is a constraint satisfaction problem with incomplete information. Full verification requires access to proprietary systems (payment processors, analytics, CRM) that sellers may not share.

**Current Approach:** Basic document requests (bank statements, revenue docs). ~31% inconsistency rate indicates the problem is largely unsolved.

### 7.3 Optimal Due Diligence Depth
**Problem:** Determine the optimal amount of due diligence given business complexity, buyer risk tolerance, and deal structure.

**Why NP-Hard:** Trade-off between diligence cost (time, money, opportunity cost) and risk reduction. The relationship between diligence depth and risk discovery is non-linear and business-specific. Optimal stopping problem with incomplete information.

**Current Approach:** AI-powered due diligence checklist (Platinum tier) provides structure but no optimization. Buyers self-determine diligence depth based on experience and risk appetite.

### 7.4 Price Discovery Without Competition
**Problem:** Establish fair market value in a single-buyer negotiation without competitive bidding.

**Why NP-Hard:** Requires estimating the distribution of buyer valuations from limited comparable data. Each business is unique; multiples vary by 2x–7x within the same category. No mechanism to surface the willingness-to-pay distribution.

**Current Approach:** Seller sets asking price; buyer negotiates downward. No auction mechanism, no competing bids, no price discovery. Result: 30–50% below asking is common; no upward pressure.

### 7.5 Fraud Detection at Scale
**Problem:** Identify fraudulent or misrepresented listings among thousands of self-reported businesses.

**Why NP-Hard:** Adversarial classification problem. Sellers have incentive to inflate metrics; detection requires ground-truth verification that is expensive and incomplete. False positives (rejecting legitimate listings) and false negatives (accepting fraudulent ones) have asymmetric costs.

**Current Approach:** Light vetting by platform team + buyer self-verification. No systematic fraud detection engine. ~31% inconsistency rate suggests the problem is largely unaddressed.
